# Chapter 15: Rules & Ethics

> The regulators, prohibited activities, ethical duties, and the catalog of rules reps must follow — Reg BI, communications, gifts, outside business activities, licensing/CE, record retention. Section 4 of the FINRA outline (9%) plus most of Section 3's "prohibited activities" come from here. Lots of memorizable numbers.

---

## 15.1 The regulators

### Securities and Exchange Commission (SEC)

The **federal government agency** (created by the Securities Exchange Act of 1934) with ultimate authority over the US securities industry. Five commissioners appointed by the President, 5-year staggered terms, no more than three from one party. The SEC:

- Enforces the federal securities laws (1933, 1934, 1939 Trust Indenture, 1940 Investment Company and Investment Advisers Acts, Sarbanes-Oxley, Dodd-Frank).
- Reviews registration statements and public company filings (EDGAR).
- Registers and oversees broker-dealers, investment advisers, exchanges, SROs, transfer agents and clearing agencies.
- Oversees and must approve SRO rules; can discipline SROs and their members.
- Brings civil enforcement actions (fines, disgorgement, injunctions, bars); refers criminal cases to the **Department of Justice**.

### Self-regulatory organizations (SROs)

Membership organizations that write and enforce rules for their members under SEC supervision.

**FINRA (Financial Industry Regulatory Authority)** — the largest SRO, formed in 2007 from the NASD and NYSE Regulation. A **non-government, non-profit** organization. Nearly every US broker-dealer and its registered representatives must be FINRA members/associated persons. FINRA:

- Writes and enforces rules for member firms and their associated persons (conduct rules, sales practice rules, advertising).
- Registers and tests reps and principals (administers the SIE, Series 6, 7, 24, etc.) and runs **CRD** (Central Registration Depository) and **BrokerCheck**.
- Examines member firms; investigates and disciplines violators (fines, suspensions, bars, expulsion of firms).
- Operates the largest securities **arbitration and mediation** forum.
- Provides market surveillance and trade reporting (TRACE, TRFs, ORF, OATS/CAT).
- FINRA's rulebook: the **Conduct Rules** (business practices), **Uniform Practice Code** (settlement, deliveries, ex-dates between members), **Code of Procedure** (how disciplinary cases are handled — Department of Enforcement, hearing panels, appeals to the National Adjudicatory Council, then the SEC, then federal court), and the **Code of Arbitration Procedure** (customer and industry disputes).

**FINRA's By-Laws** establish membership, registration and **statutory disqualification** (persons convicted of certain crimes, barred by regulators, etc., may not associate with a member without special approval).

**Exchanges** — NYSE, Nasdaq, Cboe and others are SROs for their own markets and members (listing standards, trading rules).

**MSRB (Municipal Securities Rulemaking Board)** — created by the 1975 amendments to the 1934 Act; writes rules for **broker-dealers, bank dealers and municipal advisors** in municipal securities (fair dealing, suitability, gifts, pay-to-play, disclosure). The MSRB has **no enforcement power** and **no jurisdiction over issuers** — its rules are enforced by **FINRA** (for securities firms), the **SEC**, and **bank regulators** (for bank dealers). Municipal issuers are exempt from SEC registration, but the anti-fraud rules still apply.

### Broker-dealers vs. investment advisers

| | Broker-dealer / registered rep | Investment adviser / IAR |
|---|---|---|
| Business | Buying and selling securities for customers (commissions, markups) | Giving advice about securities for a **fee** (asset-based, hourly, flat) |
| Regulator | SEC + FINRA (+ states) | SEC (≥ $100M AUM) or state; no SRO |
| Standard of conduct | **Regulation Best Interest** (for recommendations to retail customers); suitability for institutional | **Fiduciary** duty at all times |
| Registration form | BD (firm), U4 (rep) | ADV (firm), U4 (IAR) |
| Exams | SIE + Series 6/7/etc. | Series 65 or 66 |

Firms are often **dually registered** as both.

## 15.2 Prohibited activities

### 15.2.1 Market manipulation

Creating a false or misleading appearance of trading activity or prices. Prohibited under Section 9 and 10(b)/Rule 10b-5 of the 1934 Act and FINRA Rule 2020.

| Practice | Description |
|---|---|
| **Wash trades (wash sales)** | Buying and selling the same security at the same time with no change in beneficial ownership, to create the appearance of volume. (Different from the tax "wash sale rule.") |
| **Matched orders** | Two or more parties coordinate buys and sells at the same price/time to create artificial activity or price. |
| **Painting the tape** | A series of trades/reports designed to show activity or move the price. |
| **Marking the close / marking the open** | Trading near the close to manipulate the closing price (affects option values, margin, fund NAVs). |
| **Pump and dump** | Hyping a (usually thinly traded) stock with false or misleading statements, then selling into the inflated price. |
| **Front-running** | Trading ahead of a known large customer order or an upcoming research report to profit from the expected price move. |
| **Spoofing / layering** | Entering orders with no intent to execute, to move the price, then cancelling. |
| **Capping / pegging** | Trading to hold a stock's price down (capping) or up (pegging) — often around option expirations. |
| **Rumor-mongering** | Spreading false rumors to affect a price. |
| **Short-sale abuses** | Naked short selling without a locate; "bear raids." |
| **Interpositioning** | Inserting an unnecessary third party between the firm and the best market, raising the customer's cost. |
| **Backing away** | A market maker refusing to honor its firm quote. |
| **Freeriding** | In a cash account, buying and selling a security without ever paying for it (using sale proceeds to cover the purchase) — results in a 90-day freeze. |
| **Trading ahead** | A firm trading for its own account ahead of a customer's limit order at the same or better price (Manning). |

The **only permitted "manipulation"** is a disclosed **stabilizing bid** during an underwriting at or below the public offering price.

### 15.2.2 Unethical activities

Sales-practice violations that hurt customers; grounds for FINRA discipline and often civil liability.

| Practice | Description |
|---|---|
| **Churning (excessive trading)** | Trading a customer's account excessively to generate commissions, relative to the customer's objectives and resources. Red flags: high turnover, cost-to-equity ratio, in-and-out trading. Especially scrutinized in **discretionary** accounts. The rep's profit, not the customer's loss, is the measure. |
| **Unsuitable recommendations** | Recommending a security or strategy that doesn't fit the customer's profile (Reg BI / Rule 2111), e.g., speculative products for a conservative retiree, Class B shares for a $1 million purchase, VAs for an 85-year-old needing liquidity. |
| **Unauthorized trading** | Executing a trade without the customer's permission (and without written discretionary authority). Even a profitable unauthorized trade is a violation. |
| **Misrepresentation / omission** | False statements or leaving out material facts — about the security, the rep's qualifications, fees, or risks. Telling a customer the SEC "approved" a security. |
| **Guarantees against loss** | A rep or firm may **never** guarantee a customer against loss or promise a specific return (except for the issuer's own guarantees properly described, e.g., a bond's stated coupon). |
| **Sharing in a customer's account** | A rep may share in profits/losses of a customer's account **only** with **prior written approval of the firm** and the customer, and only in **proportion to the rep's investment** (exception: immediate family — proportional sharing not required). |
| **Selling away** | Participating in **private securities transactions** outside the firm (e.g., selling a friend's private placement) without **prior written notice** to the firm — and, if compensated, the firm's written **approval and supervision**. |
| **Borrowing from or lending to customers** | Prohibited unless the firm has written procedures allowing it **and** the customer is an immediate family member, a financial institution in the lending business, a registered person of the same firm, or has a personal/business relationship outside the brokerage relationship — with firm notice/approval as the procedures require. |
| **Commingling** | Mixing customer funds/securities with the firm's or the rep's own. Firms must segregate fully paid customer securities. |
| **Breakpoint sales** | Recommending a mutual fund purchase just below a breakpoint or splitting across families to avoid giving the discount (Chapter 7). |
| **Switching** | Moving a customer between fund families or VAs to generate new sales charges without a legitimate reason. |
| **Signature forgery / improper use of customer documents** | Pre-signed blank forms, altering documents, signing for a customer — serious violations. |
| **Using customer information improperly** | Disclosing non-public personal information (Reg S-P), trading on it. |
| **Failure to disclose conflicts** | Control relationships (firm controls or is controlled by the issuer), compensation arrangements, research analyst conflicts. |
| **Blanket recommendations** | Recommending the same security to all customers regardless of their profiles. |
| **Fictitious accounts / parking** | Hiding positions in other accounts to evade rules or taxes. |
| **Discrimination / high-pressure tactics / cold-calling violations** | Chapter 15.4.2 |
| **Prohibited compensation** | Accepting payment from non-members for securities business; paying continuing commissions only under written agreements. |

**Sanctions.** FINRA may censure, fine, suspend, bar individuals, expel firms, and order restitution. The SEC and state regulators can bring civil actions; the **DOJ** handles criminal prosecution (fraud carries up to **20 years** in prison and $5 million fines under Sarbanes-Oxley). Violations must be reported to the firm; the firm reports on Form U4/U5 and under Rule 4530.

### 15.2.3 Insider trading

**Insider trading** is buying or selling a security while in possession of **material, non-public information (MNPI)** about the issuer, or **tipping** such information to others who trade. Illegal under **Rule 10b-5** of the 1934 Act and the **Insider Trading and Securities Fraud Enforcement Act of 1988 (ITSFEA)**.

- **Material** — a reasonable investor would consider it important (earnings, mergers, new products, dividend changes, management changes, regulatory actions, major contracts).
- **Non-public** — not yet disseminated to the general public (via press release, SEC filing, etc.). Information remains non-public until it's been broadly released **and** the market has had time to absorb it.
- **Insiders** include not just officers, directors and 10% holders, but anyone who receives MNPI in a position of trust — lawyers, accountants, bankers, printers, consultants, family members, and **anyone who gets a tip** (the tippee), no matter how far down the chain. The **tipper** is liable even if they didn't trade. Under the **misappropriation theory**, a person who steals confidential information from any source (e.g., a lawyer trading on a client's merger plans) is also liable.
- **Trading isn't required** — passing the tip is a violation. Recommending a security based on MNPI (without saying why) is also a violation.
- **Penalties (ITSFEA):** **civil penalty up to three times the profit gained or loss avoided (treble damages)**, disgorgement, and **criminal penalties of up to 20 years in prison and $5 million per individual** (**$25 million** for firms). **Controlling persons** (the firm/supervisor) who fail to take steps to prevent insider trading can be fined the greater of $1 million (inflation-adjusted upward) or three times the profit. Whistleblowers may receive bounties (10–30% of recoveries). The statute of limitations is 5 years.
- **Firm obligations:** every broker-dealer and adviser must maintain written policies to prevent misuse of MNPI — **information barriers ("Chinese walls")** between investment banking/research and trading/sales, **restricted lists** and **watch lists**, employee trading pre-clearance and surveillance.
- **Regulation FD (Fair Disclosure)** — public companies that disclose MNPI to analysts or institutions must disclose it to the public simultaneously (if intentional) or promptly (if accidental).
- **Section 16 short-swing rule** — corporate insiders must return profits from buying and selling within 6 months, regardless of whether they had MNPI.
- **Rule 10b5-1 plans** — pre-arranged trading plans let insiders sell on a schedule without being accused of trading on MNPI.

What is **not** insider trading: trading on publicly available information, on rumors, on your own analysis ("mosaic theory" — assembling public and non-material private information), or by an insider under a 10b5-1 plan.

## 15.3 Ethical duties

### 15.3.1 Protecting vulnerable investors

**Senior and vulnerable adult protection (FINRA Rules 2165 and 4512).**

- Firms must make **reasonable efforts to obtain the name and contact information of a trusted contact person** (age 18+) when opening or updating an account. The firm may contact that person to address possible financial exploitation, confirm the customer's current contact information or health status, or identify a legal guardian/POA — but the trusted contact has no authority over the account.
- A **"specified adult"** is anyone **65 or older**, or anyone **18+** with a mental or physical impairment that makes them unable to protect their own interests.
- If the firm reasonably believes **financial exploitation** of a specified adult has occurred or is being attempted, it may place a **temporary hold on disbursements** (and, since 2022, on transactions) for up to **15 business days**, extendable by **10 more** business days (and another 30 if a regulator/agency asks). The firm must notify the trusted contact and all parties authorized on the account within 2 business days (unless they're suspected of the exploitation), open an internal review, and document everything. Firms must train associated persons on these issues.
- State laws may also require reporting to Adult Protective Services; many states have adopted the NASAA model act.
- Suitability concerns for seniors: liquidity needs, shorter horizons, cognitive decline, products with long surrender periods (VAs, non-traded REITs), concentrated positions, high-pressure "free lunch" seminars. Reps must not use misleading **senior designations** ("certified senior adviser") that imply special expertise without real credentials.
- **Diminished capacity** red flags: confusion about transactions, uncharacteristic trades, new "friends" or caregivers involved in finances, isolation from family, urgency, large gifts or withdrawals.

**Military personnel** — protected from abusive sales of periodic payment plans (Military Personnel Financial Services Protection Act); firms must not make false statements about endorsements by the armed forces.

**Privacy (Regulation S-P).** Firms must protect customers' **non-public personal information**: deliver a **privacy notice** at account opening and **annually**, give customers the right to **opt out** of sharing with non-affiliated third parties, safeguard data (written policies, encryption, secure disposal — the Safeguards and Disposal Rules), and now notify customers of data breaches within 30 days. **Regulation S-ID** requires identity-theft "red flag" programs.

### 15.3.2 Recognizing illegal activities

Associated persons are expected to know and report the following:

**Money laundering (the Bank Secrecy Act / USA PATRIOT Act).** Reviewed in Chapter 13: three stages (placement, layering, integration), **SARs for $5,000+** suspicious activity within 30 days (never tell the customer), **CTRs for cash over $10,000**, **structuring** is a crime, customer identification and OFAC screening, the firm's AML program (written, AML compliance officer named to FINRA, annual independent audit, training). Red flags: a customer who is secretive about the source of funds, wires to/from high-risk countries, deposits followed by immediate withdrawals with no investment purpose, multiple accounts with no apparent reason, transactions that make no economic sense, reluctance to provide ID, third-party payments.

**Cash reporting.** A firm receiving more than **$10,000 in cash** in one transaction (or related transactions) files a CTR; the definition of cash includes currency and certain monetary instruments. Reps should also know that personal checks and wire transfers are not "cash" for CTR purposes but can still be suspicious.

**Financial exploitation of seniors** (15.3.1).

**Insider trading** (15.2.3) and **market manipulation** (15.2.1).

**Fraud indicators:** Ponzi schemes (returns paid from new investors' money; promises of consistent high returns regardless of market), affinity fraud (targeting a religious/ethnic group), pump-and-dumps, advance-fee schemes, unregistered offerings sold with guarantees, and offshore "prime bank" schemes. Red flags: guaranteed returns, unregistered persons/products, secrecy, pressure to act fast, difficulty getting paid out, missing or unaudited statements.

**Cybersecurity.** Phishing, account takeovers, identity theft. Firms must have written cybersecurity policies, protect customer data, verify wire/disbursement requests (callbacks), and report breaches.

**Whistleblowing.** The Dodd-Frank SEC whistleblower program pays **10–30%** of sanctions over $1 million to people who provide original information; retaliation is prohibited. Associated persons who learn of violations must report them internally; firms must have procedures (and a hotline) for this.

**Reporting obligations of associated persons.** Reps must promptly report (so the firm can update Form U4 within 30 days): arrests/charges/convictions for felonies or securities-related misdemeanors, regulatory actions, customer complaints alleging fraud or theft, bankruptcies, judgments/liens, being named in investigations, and outside business activities. Failure to update a U4 is itself a violation.

### 15.4.2 Telephone Consumer Protection Act

The **TCPA of 1991** (FCC rules, implemented in FINRA Rule 3230) regulates **cold calls** (unsolicited telephone solicitations to people with no existing relationship):

- Calls only between **8:00 am and 9:00 pm** in the **called party's** local time zone (unless the person has given prior consent or is an existing customer).
- The caller must give their **name, the firm's name, the firm's address or telephone number**, and that the purpose is to sell securities.
- Firms must maintain a **firm-specific do-not-call list** (honor requests for at least **5 years**) and must check the **National Do-Not-Call Registry** at least every **31 days** before calling; numbers on the registry may not be called unless there's an established business relationship (within the prior **18 months**) or prior written consent (within 3 months of an inquiry).
- **No auto-dialed or pre-recorded calls** to cell phones without prior express consent; no unsolicited **fax** advertisements.
- Firms must train callers and keep records. Calls made in violation expose the firm to fines of $500–$1,500 per call.
- Exemptions: calls to existing customers (relationship within 18 months), calls to persons who gave written consent, calls by a rep on behalf of a tax-exempt non-profit, and calls not making a sales pitch.

### 15.4.3 Public communications

FINRA Rule 2210 classifies communications by audience and sets approval, filing and content standards.

| Category | Definition | Approval | Filing with FINRA |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Correspondence** | Written/electronic communication to **25 or fewer retail investors** within any 30-day period | Supervisory **review** (need not be pre-approved; firms must have procedures for review of incoming and outgoing) | No |
| **Retail communication** | Written/electronic communication to **more than 25 retail investors** within 30 days (ads, sales literature, websites, social media posts, seminars scripts, form letters, TV/radio, mutual fund literature) | **Prior approval by a registered principal** before use (with limited exceptions for reuse of approved material) | Some types must be filed: new firms (first year) file **10 business days before first use**; investment company (mutual fund, VA), DPP, CMO and options material filed **within 10 business days of first use**; free-writing prospectuses may require filing |
| **Institutional communication** | Communication distributed **only to institutional investors** (banks, insurers, investment companies, RIAs, entities with ≥ $50 million assets, government entities, employee benefit plans with ≥ 100 participants, FINRA members) | Written procedures; need not be pre-approved by a principal (but may be) | No |

**Content standards (all communications).** Must be **fair and balanced**, based on principles of fair dealing and good faith; must not omit material facts; no exaggerated, unwarranted, promissory or misleading statements; **no predictions or projections of performance** (except hypothetical illustrations in certain tools, e.g., VA illustrations at a 0% and ≤ 12% rate); must disclose the firm's name; comparisons must be fair; testimonials must disclose if paid and that they may not be representative; research reports and recommendations must disclose conflicts (the firm's position, investment banking relationships, analyst's holdings); must not imply SEC/FINRA approval.

**Specific items.**

- **Mutual fund performance** must show standardized 1-, 5- and 10-year average annual total returns as of the most recent quarter, with the maximum sales charge reflected, plus the "past performance" disclaimer. **Rankings** must be from independent sources and disclose the category and time period. **Yield** quoted as SEC standardized yield.
- **Options communications** must be preceded or accompanied by the ODD unless they're limited to general descriptions; approved by a Registered Options Principal.
- **Social media:** static content (profile, background) = retail communication requiring principal approval; interactive/real-time posts (tweets, chat) = treated like correspondence (supervised, retained) but must still be fair and not misleading. Reps may not use personal social media for business unless the firm supervises and retains it. Firms must retain all business communications (including texts, if used for business) for 3 years.
- **Recommendations in communications** trigger disclosures of the price at the time, the firm's market-making status and ownership, and the availability of supporting information.
- **Public appearances** (seminars, TV interviews, radio) — not pre-approval unless scripted, but subject to supervision and content standards; written materials used at a seminar are retail communications.
- **Electronic communications/email** — correspondence; firms must retain and review a sample.
- **Record retention for communications:** **3 years** (first 2 in an easily accessible place), including the name of the approving principal and the dates of first and last use.
- Research reports have extra rules (Rule 2241): analysts' independence from investment banking, quiet periods after IPOs (10 days) and before/after lock-up expirations, disclosure of ratings distribution and price charts.
- **Testimonials and "endorsements"** in investment adviser advertising are now permitted under the SEC's Marketing Rule with disclosures.
- Using **FINRA's name**: a firm may say it is a FINRA member and must display the FINRA hyperlink on its website, but may not imply FINRA endorsement.
- A **"guarantee"** language or "no risk" claims are prohibited; "**principal-protected**" products must explain the issuer's credit risk.

### 15.4.4 Proxy rules

A **proxy** is a written authorization that allows a shareholder's shares to be voted at a meeting without attending. Proxy solicitation is regulated by the SEC under the 1934 Act (Section 14):

- Companies must file **proxy statements** with the SEC and send them to shareholders before soliciting votes; they disclose the matters to be voted on (election of directors, auditor ratification, mergers, executive compensation "say-on-pay," shareholder proposals), the board's recommendations, and executive compensation.
- Shares held in **street name** are legally owned by the broker-dealer. The firm must **forward proxy materials and annual reports** to the beneficial owners (the issuer pays the costs), and must vote as the customer directs. If the customer **doesn't respond** by a deadline (typically 10 days before the meeting), the firm may vote the shares **only on routine matters** (uncontested director elections are no longer "routine"; auditor ratification is) — not on contested elections, mergers, or compensation.
- **Proxy fights (contests)** — competing groups solicit proxies for different board slates; the dissidents must also file with the SEC. Firms must forward dissident materials if the dissidents pay.
- Shareholder proposals meeting SEC thresholds must be included in the proxy.
- A shareholder may revoke a proxy by giving a later proxy or by voting in person.
- Registered reps may not **solicit proxies** for a company outside the normal forwarding process without the firm's approval; they also may not vote customer shares themselves.

### 15.4.5 Licenses & CE

**Registration.** To work in the securities business as a rep or principal you must be **associated with a FINRA member firm**, pass the required exams, and be registered by the firm through **Form U4** filed on **CRD**. Form U4 collects personal history, employment (10 years), residences (5 years), disciplinary history, criminal history, bankruptcies, liens, customer complaints, outside business activities; it requires **fingerprinting** and a **background check** by the firm. Any material change must be amended **within 30 days**. **Form U5** is filed within 30 days when a rep leaves; the firm must give the rep a copy and must be truthful (rep can't be forced to sign a U5). Registration transfers to a new firm via a new U4; the new firm reviews the U5 within 60 days of filing a U4.

Passing the SIE alone does **not** make you registered or allow you to do any securities business. |
| **Series 6** | Investment company and variable contracts rep — mutual funds, variable annuities/life, UITs; **cannot** sell individual stocks/bonds or options |
| **Series 7** | General securities rep — virtually all products (stocks, bonds, options, funds, munis, DPPs, VAs); not commodities/futures |

**Waiting periods for failed exams:** **30 days** after the first and second failures; **180 days** after the third and each subsequent failure (applies to SIE and top-off exams).

**Who must register.** Anyone who solicits, sells, or gives advice on securities, or supervises those who do, must be registered. **Exempt:** purely **clerical/ministerial** employees (though they can't take orders or discuss investments), persons whose functions are related solely to the firm's non-securities business, and persons who only handle transactions on an exchange floor (register with the exchange). A person can be registered only with the firm(s) that sponsor them (dual registration requires both firms' consent). **Unregistered persons may not** receive transaction-based compensation, take customer orders, qualify prospects, discuss products, or be paid referral fees beyond a nominal fixed amount — and reps may not **share commissions** with unregistered persons (including unregistered assistants and, after they've left the industry, former reps except under a pre-existing written continuing commission agreement).

**Permissive registration** — firms may now register employees (e.g., back office) even if they don't perform registered functions, subject to the same rules and CE.

**Maintaining qualifications (MQP).** A person whose registration lapses can keep their exam qualifications **up to 5 years** by completing annual CE while unregistered (Financial Services Affiliate Waiver and the MQP program). Otherwise, registration lapses after **2 years** out of the industry and the exams must be retaken (the SIE itself stays valid 4 years).

**Continuing education (FINRA Rule 1240).**

- **Regulatory Element** — FINRA's online training on regulatory, compliance and ethics topics, now required **annually** (by December 31) for every registered person; failure makes the registration **inactive** (can't do business or get paid) until completed; after 2 years inactive, the registration is terminated.
- **Firm Element** — the **firm's own annual training program**, based on an annual **needs analysis** and a written training plan, covering products, sales practices, regulatory developments and ethics; required of all registered persons who have customer contact or supervise; the firm keeps records.

**Statutory disqualification.** A person is disqualified from associating with a member if, among other things, within the past **10 years** they were convicted of **any felony** or a **securities/money-related misdemeanor** (fraud, theft, forgery, perjury, bribery), are subject to a regulator's bar/suspension or expulsion, or willfully made a false statement on a registration application. A firm wanting to hire a disqualified person must get FINRA's approval (an "eligibility proceeding," Form MC-400).

**Fingerprinting:** all registered persons and most employees handling cash/securities must be fingerprinted (checked against FBI records).

### 15.4.6 Registered representative rules

**Regulation Best Interest (Reg BI).** When a broker-dealer or rep makes a **recommendation** of a security, strategy, or account type (including rollovers) to a **retail customer**, they must act in the customer's **best interest** at the time of the recommendation, **without placing the financial or other interest of the firm or rep ahead of the customer's**. Four obligations:

1. **Disclosure** — before or at the time of the recommendation, disclose in writing all material facts about the relationship and the recommendation: capacity (BD vs. IA), fees and costs, type and scope of services, **conflicts of interest**, and limitations (e.g., proprietary products only). The **Form CRS (customer relationship summary)** — a short plain-English document — must be delivered to retail customers at or before the earliest of a recommendation, order, or account opening.
2. **Care** — understand the risks, rewards and costs of the recommendation (reasonable-basis), have a reasonable basis to believe it's in the **specific customer's** best interest based on their investment profile (customer-specific), and not recommend a **series** of transactions that is excessive (quantitative — churning). Cost is always a factor, though not the only one.
3. **Conflict of interest** — the firm must have written policies to **identify and disclose (or eliminate)** conflicts; must **mitigate** conflicts that create incentives for reps to place their interests ahead of the customer's; must disclose and mitigate limitations like proprietary-only menus; and must **eliminate** sales contests, quotas, bonuses and non-cash compensation based on sales of **specific securities or types of securities** within a limited period.
4. **Compliance** — written policies and procedures reasonably designed to achieve compliance with Reg BI.

Reg BI does **not** make a broker-dealer a fiduciary on an ongoing basis (no duty to monitor unless agreed), and it doesn't apply to institutional customers (FINRA's **suitability rule 2111** still covers them) or to unsolicited trades. The three suitability components (reasonable-basis, customer-specific, quantitative) still apply under Rule 2111.

**Investment profile** considered: age, other investments, financial situation and needs, tax status, objectives, experience, time horizon, liquidity needs, risk tolerance, and anything else the customer discloses.

**Know-your-customer (Rule 2090):** use reasonable diligence to know the essential facts about every customer and the authority of each person acting for them, at opening and throughout the relationship.

**Outside business activities (Rule 3270).** A registered person must give **prior written notice** to the firm before engaging in **any** business activity outside the firm for compensation (a second job, a board seat, rental real estate business, selling insurance through another entity). The firm evaluates whether it interferes with the rep's duties or could be viewed by customers as part of the firm's business and may limit or prohibit it. Passive investments aren't OBAs.

**Private securities transactions / selling away (Rule 3280).** Any securities transaction **outside the regular scope** of the rep's employment requires **prior written notice** to the firm describing the transaction, the rep's role, and whether compensation is involved. If the rep will be **compensated**, the firm must **approve in writing** and then **supervise and record** the transaction on its books as if it were the firm's own. If **no compensation**, the firm must acknowledge the notice and may impose conditions. Selling unregistered private placements or a friend's startup stock to customers without this is the classic "selling away" violation.

**Personal accounts at other firms (Rule 3210).** A registered person who opens an account at **another** broker-dealer (or has a beneficial interest in one — including a spouse's/dependent's account) must get **prior written consent** of their **employer**, and must notify the executing firm that they're associated with a member. The employer may request **duplicate confirmations and statements**. Accounts limited to mutual funds, VAs, UITs, or 529 plans are exempt.

**Gifts and gratuities (Rule 3220).** Business-related gifts to persons associated with **another member firm or a customer** are limited to **$100 per person per year** and must be recorded by the firm. Exclusions: **business entertainment** (meals, event tickets) where the giver **attends** and it's not so lavish as to be improper, promotional items of nominal value (logo pens, mugs), and personal gifts (wedding/baby gifts) clearly not business-related. **Non-cash compensation** from product sponsors (e.g., mutual fund wholesalers) is governed by separate rules: occasional meals/tickets OK, training/education meetings may be paid for only with firm approval and no guests, and sales contests must be based on total production across all products of a type, not a specific sponsor's product.

**Borrowing from or lending to customers (Rule 3240).** Prohibited unless the firm has written procedures permitting it **and** the customer is: an **immediate family member**; a **financial institution** in the business of lending; a **registered person of the same firm**; a person with a **personal relationship** outside the brokerage relationship; or a **business relationship** outside the brokerage relationship. The last three categories require **notice to and approval by the firm** in writing; immediate family and institutions do not (unless the firm requires it).

**Sharing in customer accounts (Rule 2150).** Only with **prior written authorization** of the firm and the customer, and the rep's share must be **proportionate** to their financial contribution (except with immediate family members). Guaranteeing against loss is **always** prohibited. The firm may not use customer securities without authorization (margin agreement).

**Discretionary authority** — written authorization + principal approval + review (Chapter 13).

**Commissions and compensation.** May be paid only to registered persons through the firm; cannot be paid to unregistered persons or to customers; a departed rep may receive continuing commissions only under a bona fide written contract entered into before leaving. **Fee-based accounts** must be appropriate for the customer's trading activity. Reps may not accept compensation from customers directly for securities advice without the firm's knowledge.

**Political contributions (pay-to-play).** MSRB **G-37** (muni dealers — $250 limit per election to candidates you can vote for; two-year ban) and SEC Rule 206(4)-5 (investment advisers — $350/$150 limits) restrict contributions to officials who can influence the award of business; FINRA Rule 2030 applies similar limits to member firms soliciting government entities for advisers.

**Use of titles.** A rep may not use misleading professional designations; "financial adviser/planner" titles require disclosure; "senior specialist" designations without substance are prohibited.

**Supervision (Rule 3110).** Every firm must have a **written supervisory procedures (WSP)** manual, designate principals responsible for supervising each type of business and each **office of supervisory jurisdiction (OSJ)**, review all correspondence and transactions, conduct an **annual compliance meeting** (can be virtual), inspect OSJs annually and branch offices periodically (non-supervisory branches every 3 years, supervisory branches annually), and conduct an **annual compliance certification** by the CEO. Firms must also have a **compliance officer** and an **AML officer**. Registered reps are supervised by principals; a principal must approve new accounts, discretionary orders, correspondence samples, retail communications and options accounts.

### 15.4.7 Record retention requirements

SEC Rules 17a-3/17a-4 and FINRA Rule 4511 dictate what broker-dealers must keep and for how long. Records must be kept for the required period, with the first **2 years in an easily accessible place**, and may be stored electronically (non-rewriteable, non-erasable "WORM" format or audit-trail systems).

| Retention period | Records |
|---|---|
| **Lifetime of the firm (permanent)** | Articles of incorporation/partnership agreements, **minute books**, stock certificate books, Form BD and amendments, FINRA membership agreement |
| **6 years** | **Blotters** (daily records of all purchases, sales, receipts and deliveries of cash and securities), **general ledger**, **customer ledgers (account records)**, **stock record** (securities position ledger), **customer account information / new account forms** (6 years after the account is closed), designation records of principals |
| **5 years** | **Do-not-call records**; **AML/CIP identity-verification records** (5 years after the account closes, under the Bank Secrecy Act) |
| **4 years** | **Customer complaints** (and related records) |
| **3 years** | **Order tickets/memoranda**, **trade confirmations**, **trial balances and computations of net capital**, **employment applications and U4/U5 for terminated persons (3 years after termination)**, **communications with the public (retail communications, correspondence, institutional communications, including approval records)**, advertising/sales literature, **fingerprint cards**, **subsidiary ledgers**, **Form 144 records**, gifts & gratuities records, **customer account statements**, **compliance/supervisory records (WSPs, annual compliance reports)**, business continuity plan records, options agreements, margin agreements, **FOCUS reports**, checks/bank statements, records of associated persons' outside business activities |
| **Until account closed + 6 years** | Customer account records |

Simplified version the exam expects:

- **Forever:** organizational documents, minutes, stock certificate books.
- **6 years:** blotters, ledgers, stock record, account records/new account forms.
- **4 years:** complaints.
- **3 years:** almost everything else — order tickets, confirms, communications, U4/U5 of ex-employees, net capital computations, gifts records, fingerprint records, trial balances.
- **Regulatory element CE records** and **Firm Element plans:** 3 years. **Do-not-call:** 5 years.

**Electronic records** must have an audit trail, a backup copy, an index, and the firm must be able to download them for regulators. FINRA and the SEC may inspect any record on demand.

**Financial reports:** broker-dealers file **FOCUS reports** (monthly or quarterly) and an **annual audited financial statement** (within 60 days of fiscal year-end) with the SEC and FINRA, and must make a **statement of financial condition (balance sheet)** available to customers semiannually. Net capital requirements must be met at all times (**net capital rule**); a firm falling below must notify the SEC/FINRA immediately and stop expanding.

**Books and records on reps:** Form U4, fingerprint records, outside business activity disclosures, CE records, and a record of each office.

## Chapter 15 quick review

- **SEC** = government regulator (1934 Act); **FINRA** = largest SRO (BDs and reps; exams, CRD, BrokerCheck, arbitration); **MSRB** = muni rules only, no enforcement (FINRA/SEC/bank regulators enforce), no authority over issuers; **state Administrators** (NASAA, blue-sky, Series 63/65/66).
- BD standard: **Reg BI** (best interest for retail recommendations; Form CRS; 4 obligations: disclosure, care, conflicts, compliance; no sales contests on specific securities). IA standard: **fiduciary**.
- Manipulation: wash trades, matched orders, painting the tape, marking the close, pump-and-dump, front-running, spoofing, capping/pegging. Only legal one: stabilizing bid.
- Unethical: churning, unsuitable, unauthorized trading, misrepresentation, **guarantees against loss (never)**, sharing accounts (written approval + proportional, except family), selling away (written notice; approval if paid), borrowing/lending (family, institution, same-firm rep, personal/business relationship + firm procedures), commingling, breakpoint sales.
- **Insider trading:** MNPI; tippers and tippees liable; misappropriation theory; **treble damages**, **$5M/20 years** individuals, $25M firms; information barriers; Reg FD; Section 16 short-swing; 10b5-1 plans.
- **Seniors:** trusted contact (must attempt); specified adult = 65+ or impaired 18+; temporary hold on disbursements **15 business days + 10 (+30)**.
- AML: SAR $5,000 within 30 days; CTR > $10,000 cash; placement → layering → integration; structuring illegal; AML officer; annual audit.
- **TCPA:** 8 am–9 pm called party's time; identify self/firm/contact; firm DNC list + national registry (check every 31 days); 18-month existing relationship exemption.
- **Communications:** correspondence ≤ 25 retail/30 days (review); retail > 25 (principal pre-approval; some filed — new firms 10 days before; fund/VA/DPP/CMO within 10 days of use); institutional (procedures). Fair and balanced, no predictions, no guarantees; fund performance 1/5/10 years at max load; records 3 years.
- Proxies: firm forwards materials for street-name shares; votes uninstructed shares only on routine matters.
- **SIE:** anyone 18+, 75 questions, 105 minutes, 70 to pass, valid 4 years, co-requisite — not a registration. Series 6 (funds/VAs), 7 (general), 63/65/66 (state), 24 (principal). Fail waits: 30/30/180 days. U4 within 30 days of changes; U5 within 30 days of leaving; registration lapses after 2 years out (MQP up to 5). **CE:** Regulatory Element annually (inactive if missed); Firm Element annually with needs analysis. Statutory disqualification: felony or securities misdemeanor within 10 years.
- **Rep rules:** OBA → prior written **notice**; private securities transactions → notice, and **approval** if compensated; outside accounts → prior written consent + duplicate confirms; **gifts $100/person/year** (entertainment excluded if giver attends); borrowing/lending restricted; no sharing commissions with unregistered persons.
- **Records:** permanent (organizational docs, minutes); **6 years** (blotters, ledgers, stock record, account records); **4 years** (complaints); **3 years** (order tickets, confirms, communications, U4/U5 after termination, net capital, gifts); first 2 years easily accessible.
