# Chapter 13: Brokerage Accounts

> Account documentation, the types of account registrations, margin basics, options accounts, SIPC and business continuity. Section 3 of the FINRA outline (31%) draws heavily from here — especially new-account requirements, custodial/joint/discretionary accounts, Reg T, and SIPC coverage.

---

## 13.1 Fundamentals

### 13.1.1 Statements

Customer **account statements** show the account's positions, their market values, all activity (trades, deposits, withdrawals, dividends/interest, fees) for the period, and the firm's contact information.

- Must be sent at least **quarterly** (every calendar quarter) for any account with activity or a balance; in practice, **monthly** when there's activity.
- Must include a statement advising the customer to **report any inaccuracies promptly** to the firm (and to the carrying firm, if introduced).
- Penny stock positions must show the market value and the firm's estimate.
- Statements for **DPPs and non-traded REITs** must show a per-share estimated value.
- Customers may ask that statements be held (e.g., while traveling) for a limited time (up to 3 months with a written request and a valid reason); statements may not be sent to the rep instead of the customer.
- Electronic delivery requires the customer's **affirmative consent**.

### 13.1.2 Trade confirmations

A **confirmation** is sent for every transaction **at or before completion of the transaction (the settlement date)**. Required contents (SEC Rule 10b-10):

- Trade date and (if different) settlement date; security description; quantity; **price**.
- Whether the firm acted as **agent** (commission disclosed) or **principal** (markup/markdown for most retail bond trades; for equities, that it acted as principal).
- For bonds: **yield** (yield to worst for callable bonds), call features, and whether the bond is unrated.
- Whether the firm receives **payment for order flow**.
- For mutual funds: sales charge information or reference to the prospectus.

Customers should receive confirms directly; a customer may direct duplicate confirms to a third party (e.g., an adviser) in writing.

### 13.1.3 Customer complaints

A **complaint** is any **written** (including electronic — email, text, social media message) statement from a customer or someone acting for a customer alleging a grievance involving the firm or an associated person's activities.

- Must be reported to the firm's **compliance/principal**, logged, investigated and responded to; the firm keeps a **complaint file** at the OSJ.
- Firms must report statistical summaries of written customer complaints to FINRA **quarterly** (Rule 4530) and must report any complaint alleging theft/forgery/misappropriation promptly (within 30 days).
- Records of complaints are retained for **4 years**.
- A rep must **never** settle a complaint privately, pay the customer out of pocket, or hide a complaint from the firm.
- Customer complaints are also disclosed on the rep's **Form U4/U5** and on **BrokerCheck** in many cases.
- Disputes between customers and firms are generally resolved through **FINRA arbitration** (most new-account agreements contain a **pre-dispute arbitration clause**, which must be highlighted and explained). Arbitration awards are final and binding, with very limited appeal; claims must be filed within **6 years** of the event. **Mediation** is a voluntary, non-binding alternative. Disputes between firms or between a firm and its reps must go to arbitration (industry disputes). Class actions are excluded from arbitration.

## 13.2 New accounts

### 13.2.1 Required customer information

To open an account, FINRA Rule 4512 and the USA PATRIOT Act's **Customer Identification Program (CIP)** require the firm to obtain, at a minimum:

- **Name** and **address** (residence; a P.O. box alone is not acceptable).
- **Date of birth** (must be of legal age — generally 18 — to open an account in their own name).
- **Taxpayer ID / Social Security number** (or passport/ID for non-US persons).
- Whether the customer is of **legal age**.
- The name of the **registered representative** responsible for the account and the signature of the **principal** who approved it.
- Whether the customer is **employed by another broker-dealer or FINRA member** (if so, the employer must be notified and may require duplicate confirms/statements).
- Whether the customer is a **control person / insider** of a public company (affects Rule 144 handling).
- For **cash accounts**, the customer's **signature is not required**; for margin and options accounts, signed agreements are required.

The firm must **verify the customer's identity** (via documents like a driver's license/passport or non-documentary means like credit bureaus) within a reasonable time after opening, keep records of the verification, and check the customer against the **OFAC** Specially Designated Nationals list.

The firm must also provide the customer with a copy of the **account record** (and any updates) at opening and at least every **36 months** thereafter, and must give new customers a written notice about **SIPC** and about **FINRA BrokerCheck** (with FINRA's hotline number and website).

### 13.2.2 Optional customer information

FINRA's suitability rule (and Reg BI for recommendations to retail customers) requires firms to make a **reasonable effort** to collect the customer's **investment profile** — but the customer may decline to provide it, in which case the rep must not make recommendations without a reasonable basis:

- **Investment objectives** (growth, income, preservation of capital, speculation).
- **Financial situation** — annual income, net worth (liquid net worth), other investments.
- **Tax status / bracket.**
- **Risk tolerance.**
- **Time horizon** and **liquidity needs**.
- **Investment experience.**
- **Age** and employment/occupation.
- **Trusted contact person** (FINRA Rule 4512 requires firms to *attempt* to obtain the name of a trusted contact — a person age 18+ the firm may contact about possible exploitation or diminished capacity; the customer may decline).

If a customer refuses to give financial information, the account can still be opened (for unsolicited trades); the rep simply can't justify recommendations.

### 13.2.3 Account opening

**Steps.**

1. Rep collects the required/optional information on the **new account form**.
2. A **principal** reviews and **approves** the account (signature) — before or promptly after the first trade. No principal signature = no account.
3. For **margin** accounts: customer signs the **margin agreement** (credit agreement, hypothecation agreement, loan consent) and receives the margin risk disclosure.
4. For **options** accounts: customer receives the **ODD**, a Registered Options Principal (ROP) approves, and the customer signs the **options agreement within 15 days** of approval.
5. For **discretionary** accounts: written **power of attorney/trading authorization** from the customer and principal approval.
6. Firm delivers the account record copy, SIPC and BrokerCheck notices, and (for margin) the margin disclosure.

**Numbered accounts.** A customer may use a number or symbol instead of a name on the account for privacy, but the firm must keep a **signed statement of ownership** on file.

**Cash account** — the customer must pay in full for each purchase by the Reg T deadline (S+2). Most accounts. **Margin account** — the customer borrows part of the purchase price from the firm (13.4).

**Restrictions and red flags.** Firms may refuse to open an account. Accounts for **employees of other FINRA firms** require prior written notice to the employer. Accounts for **minors** must be custodial. **Institutional** accounts (banks, insurers, RIAs, entities with ≥ $50 million) have modified suitability obligations.

**Anti-money laundering (AML) at account opening.** Firms must have an AML program (written policies, a designated AML compliance officer, annual independent testing, ongoing training, and customer due diligence). **Suspicious activity reports (SARs)** are filed with **FinCEN** for suspicious transactions of **$5,000+** within **30 days**, and the customer must not be told. **Currency transaction reports (CTRs)** are filed for cash transactions over **$10,000** in a day. Money laundering has three stages: **placement** (cash enters the system), **layering** (complex transfers to hide the trail), **integration** (funds return looking legitimate). **Structuring** (breaking deposits into sub-$10,000 pieces to avoid CTRs) is illegal.

## 13.3 Account registrations

### 13.3.1 Individual accounts

One owner, who alone can trade, deposit and withdraw. On death, the account becomes part of the owner's **estate** (probate) unless a **transfer-on-death (TOD)** designation names a beneficiary, which passes the assets directly. Only the owner can give instructions (unless a third-party trading authorization is on file).

### 13.3.2 Joint accounts

Two or more owners; each has full authority to trade. All owners sign the joint account agreement; checks must be payable to **all** owners (or the account title), never to one owner alone. Two main forms:

| | **Joint tenants with right of survivorship (JTWROS)** | **Tenants in common (TIC / JTIC)** |
|---|---|---|
| Ownership | Equal and undivided | May be **unequal** percentages (e.g., 70/30) |
| At death of one owner | The deceased's share passes **to the surviving owner(s)** automatically, bypassing probate | The deceased's share passes to the deceased's **estate** |
| Typical users | Married couples | Business partners, unrelated investors |

Other forms: **tenancy by the entirety** (married couples in some states — creditor protection), **community property** (in community property states, assets acquired during marriage are owned 50/50).

### 13.3.3 Power of attorney

A customer may authorize a **third party** to act on the account:

- **Limited (trading) authorization** — the third party may **trade** but **not withdraw** assets.
- **Full power of attorney** — the third party may trade **and** withdraw/transfer assets.
- A **durable** power of attorney remains in effect if the customer becomes incapacitated; a non-durable POA ends at incapacity. **All POAs end at the customer's death** — the executor then controls the estate.
- Must be in **writing**, signed by the customer, and kept on file. The firm may require its own form.

### 13.3.4 Discretionary accounts

An account in which the **registered representative** decides **what security, whether to buy or sell, and how much** without checking with the customer on each trade. Requirements:

- **Written trading authorization (power of attorney)** from the customer **before** any discretionary trade.
- **Principal approval** of the account, and a principal must **review each discretionary order promptly** (frequently reviewed to detect churning).
- Each order ticket must be **marked "discretionary."**
- Choosing only the **time or price** of an order the customer has already specified (security, action, amount) is **not** discretion and needs no written authority (it's a "not-held" order, good for the day).
- Discretion must still be exercised suitably; excessive trading in a discretionary account is **churning**.
- Investment advisers often have discretion under their advisory agreement; broker-dealers' reps need the firm's approval.

### 13.3.5 Custodial accounts

Accounts for **minors** (who cannot legally enter contracts) under the **Uniform Gifts to Minors Act (UGMA)** or the **Uniform Transfers to Minors Act (UTMA)** — state laws.

- **One custodian, one minor** per account. The custodian (any adult; needn't be a parent) manages the account; the **minor is the beneficial owner** and the account uses the **minor's Social Security number** (taxes are the minor's, with kiddie-tax rules).
- Gifts are **irrevocable** — once given, the money belongs to the minor and can't be taken back. Anyone may give; there's no limit, but gifts above the annual exclusion ($19,000 in 2026) have gift-tax consequences for the donor.
- The custodian has a **fiduciary duty**: invest prudently, no margin, no options writing (long options may be prohibited by firm policy), no speculative trading, may not commingle with their own assets. Custodian may be reimbursed for reasonable expenses but may not profit.
- Can be used for the minor's benefit but not for parental support obligations.
- The minor receives the assets at the **age of majority** (UGMA: 18; UTMA: **up to 21 or 25**, depending on the state) — the account is then re-registered in their name. UTMA allows a broader range of assets (real estate, etc.).
- Only the custodian may give instructions; a third-party trading authorization is not allowed; registration is "[Custodian] as custodian for [Minor] under the [State] UTMA."

### 13.3.6 Guardianship accounts

Opened for a person a court has declared unable to manage their affairs — a **minor without a custodian** (e.g., after an inheritance) or an **incompetent adult**. The **court-appointed guardian (or conservator)** controls the account and must provide the **court appointment documents**, which typically expire and must be renewed. The guardian is a fiduciary bound by the court's rules. Similar fiduciary accounts: **executor/administrator** accounts for estates (requires letters testamentary and a tax ID for the estate), **receiver** in bankruptcy.

### 13.3.7 Trust accounts

A **trust** holds assets for the benefit of **beneficiaries**, managed by a **trustee** under the **trust document**. The firm needs a copy of the trust (or a trustee certification) showing the trustee's authority, the type of trust, and the tax ID.

- **Revocable (living) trust** — the grantor can change or revoke it; assets are still in the grantor's taxable estate; avoids probate.
- **Irrevocable trust** — can't be changed; assets are removed from the grantor's estate (estate-tax planning); the trust is a separate taxpayer.
- **Testamentary trust** — created by a will at death.
- The trustee is a **fiduciary** governed by the **prudent investor rule** (diversify, reasonable risk, act solely for beneficiaries). Margin and options are generally prohibited unless the trust document specifically permits them.

### 13.3.8 Business accounts

- **Sole proprietorship** — treated like an individual account in the owner's name d/b/a the business.
- **Partnership** — the firm needs the **partnership agreement** showing which partners may trade and whether margin is permitted; general partners are liable.
- **Corporation** — requires a **corporate resolution** (naming the officers authorized to trade) and, for margin or options, the **corporate charter/bylaws** showing the corporation is permitted to do so. Corporate accounts use the company's EIN.
- **LLC** — operating agreement and resolution.
- **Unincorporated association / investment club** — agreement naming authorized persons; no breakpoints for clubs.
- **Institutional accounts** (banks, insurers, investment companies, RIAs, any entity with ≥ $50 million in assets) — a rep may rely on the institution's own judgment for suitability if the institution affirmatively says it is exercising independent judgment.

### 13.3.9 Prime brokerage accounts

Used by **hedge funds and large institutions**: the client trades through **multiple executing brokers** but **settles and custodies everything with one prime broker**, which also provides margin lending, securities lending for short sales, reporting and other services. The client signs a prime brokerage agreement; executing brokers give up trades to the prime broker for settlement. A **DVP/RVP (delivery vs. payment / receive vs. payment)** account is a related institutional arrangement in which the customer's custodian bank pays for securities on delivery — a form of cash account with no money held at the broker-dealer.

**Other account arrangements to recognize:** **fee-based (wrap) accounts** (one asset-based fee covers advice and trading — must be suitable for active traders, not buy-and-hold investors who'd pay less in commissions); **advisory accounts** (managed by an RIA); **day-trading accounts** (special disclosure and $25,000 minimum equity for pattern day traders); **TOD/POD** designations; **transfer via ACATS** (Automated Customer Account Transfer Service — the receiving firm sends a transfer initiation form; the delivering firm must validate or take exception within **1 business day** and complete the transfer within **3 business days** after validation).

## 13.4 Margin accounts

### 13.4.1 Overview

A **margin account** lets a customer **borrow part of the purchase price** of securities from the broker-dealer, using the securities as collateral. Margin magnifies both gains and losses (leverage). Rules come from three layers:

1. **The Federal Reserve — Regulation T** (under the 1934 Act): initial margin for purchases = **50%** of the purchase price for most securities; payment due by **S+2** (two business days after settlement, i.e., T+3). Also defines which securities are marginable.
2. **FINRA / exchange rules** — **minimum initial deposit** of **$2,000** (or 100% of the purchase if less) and **maintenance margin** (**25%** long, **30%** short).
3. **House rules** — firms may set stricter (higher) requirements, often 30–35% maintenance.

**Marginable securities:** exchange-listed and Nasdaq stocks, most OTC stocks on the margin list, corporate/muni/government bonds (with lower requirements), mutual funds after **30 days** of ownership (new mutual fund purchases and new issues/IPOs cannot be bought on margin until 30 days after). **Options** (except LEAPS with > 9 months) must be paid in full. Penny stocks and most low-priced OTC stocks are not marginable.

**Why margin?** Leverage — more buying power — and the ability to **sell short** (which must be done in a margin account). **Risks:** losses can exceed the initial investment; the firm can sell securities without notice (margin call) and chooses which; interest accrues on the loan; the customer isn't entitled to an extension of time on a call.

### 13.4.2 Opening a margin account

The customer signs the **margin agreement**, which has three parts:

| Agreement | Purpose |
|---|---|
| **Credit agreement** | Terms of the loan — interest rate (based on the **broker call rate** plus a spread) and how it's computed |
| **Hypothecation agreement** | Customer **pledges (hypothecates)** the securities as collateral; permits the firm to **rehypothecate** (re-pledge) up to **140% of the debit balance** to a bank to finance the loan |
| **Loan consent form** (optional) | Allows the firm to **lend the customer's securities to other customers** for short sales. Customer may decline to sign this part. |

Also required: **margin risk disclosure statement** delivered at or before opening and **annually** thereafter; principal approval; margin account securities are held in **street name**. Who **cannot** open a margin account: **custodial (UGMA/UTMA) accounts**, most fiduciary/trust accounts (unless the document permits), **retirement accounts (IRAs)**, and minors.

### 13.4.3 Deposit requirements

**Long purchase.** Reg T initial = **50%** of the market value. Minimum initial deposit = **$2,000** (FINRA), unless the full purchase price is less than $2,000 — then pay in full.

- Buy $10,000 of stock → deposit $5,000; the firm lends $5,000 (the **debit balance**).
- Buy $3,000 of stock → 50% is $1,500, but the $2,000 minimum applies → deposit **$2,000**.
- Buy $1,500 of stock → pay the full **$1,500** (can't deposit more than the price).

**Short sale.** Reg T initial = **50%** of the short sale proceeds, deposited in addition to the proceeds, which are held by the firm. Short $10,000 → the account holds $10,000 proceeds + $5,000 deposit = $15,000 **credit balance**. Minimum $2,000 applies.

**Meeting a Reg T call.** Deposit **cash** for the full amount, or deposit **fully paid marginable securities** worth **twice** the call (because the securities themselves can only be margined at 50%). Calls must be met by **S+2**; the firm may request an extension from FINRA (for exceptional circumstances), otherwise it sells out the position and freezes the account for **90 days**.

**Other initial requirements (FINRA):** Treasury securities — a small percentage of market value based on maturity (e.g., 1–6%); municipal bonds — the greater of 7% of face or 15% of market value; investment-grade corporate bonds — 20–25%.

### 13.4.4 Equity

**Long account.**

```
Long market value (LMV) − Debit balance (DR) = Equity (EQ)
```

Equity is the customer's ownership in the account — what they'd have if everything were sold and the loan repaid. The debit balance is **fixed** (except for interest and new transactions); as the market value changes, equity changes dollar for dollar.

**Example.** Buy $20,000 of stock with a $10,000 deposit → LMV $20,000, DR $10,000, EQ $10,000 (50%). If the stock rises to $24,000 → EQ $14,000. If it falls to $16,000 → EQ $6,000.

- **Excess equity (SMA — special memorandum account).** When the market value rises, equity above the 50% Reg T requirement becomes excess equity, credited to **SMA**, a line of credit the customer can withdraw as cash or use to buy more securities (**buying power = SMA × 2**). With LMV $24,000 and DR $10,000, the Reg T requirement is $12,000, so excess = $2,000 → buying power $4,000. SMA, once created, does not go away when the market falls (it can only be used if it won't violate maintenance).
- **Restricted account.** If equity falls **below 50%** (but above maintenance), the account is restricted — no Reg T call, but new purchases require 50% of the new purchase, and sales of securities require that **50% of proceeds** be retained to reduce the debit (the "retention requirement"); the other 50% may be withdrawn.

**Short account.**

```
Credit balance (CR) − Short market value (SMV) = Equity
```

Short $10,000 with $5,000 deposit → CR $15,000, SMV $10,000, EQ $5,000. If the stock **rises** to $12,000 → EQ $3,000 (losing). If it **falls** to $8,000 → EQ $7,000 (winning). The credit balance is fixed; equity moves inversely to the stock.

**Combined accounts** net the long and short sides. Interest is charged on the debit balance only.

### 13.4.5 Minimum maintenance

To keep the loan protected, equity must never fall below the **maintenance requirement**:

- **Long accounts: 25%** of the long market value (FINRA minimum; houses often use 30%+).
- **Short accounts: 30%** of the short market value (higher because losses are unlimited). Special minimums for low-priced shorts: **$2.50/share or 100%** of market value (whichever is greater) for stocks under $5; **$5/share or 30%** for stocks $5 and above.

If equity drops below maintenance, the firm issues a **maintenance (house) call**, which must be met **promptly** (firms' policies typically give a few days) by depositing cash, depositing marginable securities (worth 1÷0.75 ≈ 1.33× the call, since 25% of the deposit's value is retained as margin), or selling securities (at 25% maintenance, selling securities worth 4× the call amount cures it, because the proceeds reduce the debit). The firm may liquidate without notice.

**Shortcut formulas.**

- Long account: the market value at which a maintenance call occurs = **Debit balance ÷ 0.75** (i.e., debit ÷ (1 − 25%)). For DR $10,000: LMV below $13,333 triggers a call.
- Short account at 30%: market value at which a call occurs = **Credit balance ÷ 1.30**. For CR $15,000: SMV above $11,538 triggers a call.
- Maintenance call amount = required equity − actual equity.

**Example.** LMV $12,000, DR $10,000 → EQ $2,000. Required at 25% = $3,000. **Maintenance call = $1,000.**

**Pattern day trader rule.** A customer who makes **4 or more day trades in 5 business days** (and those trades are > 6% of total trades) in a margin account is a pattern day trader and must maintain **$25,000 minimum equity**; day-trading buying power is 4× maintenance excess.

**Portfolio margin** — a risk-based alternative for sophisticated customers with lower requirements; needs special approval.

## 13.5 Options accounts

Because options carry special risks, opening an options account has extra steps (FINRA Rule 2360):

1. The rep gathers the customer's financial information, investment experience and objectives, and determines what level of option trading is suitable (firms use approval levels: e.g., Level 1 covered calls → Level 2 long calls/puts → Level 3 spreads → Level 4 uncovered writing).
2. The customer receives the **Options Disclosure Document (ODD)** — *Characteristics and Risks of Standardized Options* — **at or before** the account is approved.
3. A **Registered Options Principal (ROP)** or a designated branch manager **approves** the account in writing (the account may not be approved by the rep alone). The approval must be documented.
4. The customer signs and returns the **options agreement** (acknowledging the ODD and agreeing to abide by position/exercise limits and the firm's rules) **within 15 days** of account approval. If not returned, the customer may **only close** existing positions — no new opening transactions.
5. Each options recommendation must be suitable, and each options order must be approved by an ROP/principal promptly.

**Uncovered writing** requires a margin account and the highest approval level; a special written statement of the risks of uncovered options must be provided. **Retirement accounts and custodial accounts** are limited to conservative strategies (covered calls, protective puts, long options as permitted).

**Position limits** cap the number of contracts on the same side of the market an investor (or group acting together) may hold in one underlying; **exercise limits** cap exercises in 5 business days. The OCC and exchanges set them (large for liquid stocks, e.g., 250,000 contracts).

## 13.6 Other account specifications

### 13.6.1 SIPC insurance

The **Securities Investor Protection Corporation (SIPC)** — a **non-profit, non-government** membership corporation created by the Securities Investor Protection Act of 1970, funded by assessments on broker-dealer members. Nearly all broker-dealers must be members.

**What SIPC does.** Protects customers if a **broker-dealer fails (goes bankrupt/insolvent)** and customer assets are missing. A trustee is appointed to liquidate the firm and return customer property. SIPC restores missing securities and cash up to the limits.

**Coverage limits:** up to **$500,000 per separate customer**, of which no more than **$250,000 may be cash**.

- Coverage is per **separate customer** (separate capacity), not per account: an individual account, a joint account, an IRA, and a custodial account at the same firm each count as a separate customer. Two individual accounts in the same name at the same firm are **combined**.
- A **margin account's** claim is measured by equity (market value less the debit balance).
- Securities are valued as of the date the trustee is appointed (the filing date).
- Claims above SIPC limits become general creditor claims against the failed firm.

**What SIPC does NOT cover.**

- **Market losses** (a decline in the value of investments) — ever.
- Commodity/futures contracts, fixed annuities, currency, precious metals, and investment contracts not registered as securities.
- Unregistered investment contracts, Ponzi losses beyond what was on the books, and claims against firms that aren't SIPC members (e.g., investment advisers alone, banks).
- Losses from bad advice, unsuitable recommendations, or fraud (customers pursue arbitration for those).

**Disclosure:** firms must display the SIPC logo and give new customers written notice of SIPC coverage and how to contact SIPC (website/phone), and repeat the notice annually.

**Compare: FDIC** insures bank deposits (checking, savings, CDs) up to **$250,000** per depositor per bank per ownership category; a government agency. SIPC is not FDIC — brokerage sweep accounts into bank deposits get FDIC, not SIPC, coverage.

### 13.6.2 Business continuity plans

FINRA Rule 4370 requires every member firm to create, maintain and annually review a **business continuity plan (BCP)** describing how the firm will meet its obligations to customers in a **significant business disruption** (natural disaster, terrorist attack, pandemic, power/communications failure, cyberattack).

The plan must address at minimum: data backup and recovery (hard copy and electronic); all mission-critical systems; financial and operational assessments; alternate communications between the firm and customers, and between the firm and employees; alternate physical location of employees; critical business constituent, bank and counterparty impact; regulatory reporting; communications with regulators; and **how the firm will assure customers prompt access to their funds and securities** if the firm can't continue (e.g., if the firm is unable to continue, transferring accounts to another firm).

- Must be approved by a member of senior management (a registered principal) and updated for material changes.
- Firms must **disclose a summary** of the BCP to customers **in writing at account opening**, post it on the firm's website, and mail it on request.
- Firms designate **two emergency contact persons** (both members of senior management, at least one a registered principal) whose information is given to FINRA and updated promptly.

## Chapter 13 quick review

- Statements at least quarterly (monthly with activity); confirms at or before settlement, showing capacity and commission/markup. Written complaints go to the principal; kept 4 years; disputes to FINRA arbitration (6-year eligibility window).
- **Required at opening:** name, address, DOB/legal age, SSN/TIN, rep's name, principal approval, employment with another BD, insider status; verify identity (CIP), check OFAC. Suitability info (objectives, income, net worth, tax, risk tolerance, horizon, experience) is sought but may be refused. Trusted contact must be *requested*.
- Account record copy at opening and every 36 months; SIPC and BrokerCheck notices.
- AML: SAR for $5,000+ suspicious (30 days, don't tell customer); CTR for cash > $10,000; placement → layering → integration; structuring is illegal.
- **JTWROS** → survivor gets it (equal ownership); **TIC** → estate gets it (unequal OK). Checks payable to all owners.
- Limited POA = trade only; full POA = trade + withdraw; durable survives incapacity; all end at death.
- **Discretionary** = rep chooses security/action/amount → written authorization + principal approval + review + ticket marked. Time/price only is not discretion.
- **UGMA/UTMA:** one custodian, one minor, minor's SSN, irrevocable gifts, no margin, fiduciary, transferred at majority (18/21/25).
- Guardian needs court papers; trustee needs trust document (prudent investor rule); corporation needs resolution (plus charter for margin/options).
- Prime brokerage: multiple executing brokers, one settlement/custody broker.
- **Margin:** Reg T 50% (Fed), $2,000 minimum and maintenance 25% long / 30% short (FINRA); three agreements (credit, hypothecation — rehypothecate 140% of debit, loan consent optional); risk disclosure at opening and annually; no margin for custodial, IRA, new mutual funds/IPOs for 30 days.
- LMV − DR = EQ; CR − SMV = EQ. Maintenance call when LMV < DR ÷ 0.75. Excess equity → SMA; buying power = 2 × SMA. Pattern day trader: $25,000.
- **Options account:** ODD before approval; ROP approves; options agreement signed within 15 days (else closing-only).
- **SIPC:** $500,000 per separate customer, max $250,000 cash; covers BD failure, **not market losses**; not commodities/fixed annuities; non-government. FDIC: $250,000 bank deposits.
- **BCP:** annual review, senior management approval, summary disclosed to customers at opening and on the website, two emergency contacts.
