# Chapter 2: Preferred Stock

> **In one line:** Preferred stock is equity that acts like a bond — a fixed dividend with priority over common, but price moves inversely to interest rates.

## The big ideas

1. **A hybrid** — legally equity (dividends not a legal obligation, no maturity), economically a fixed-income security, so it sits between bonds and common in every comparison.
2. **"Preferred" = first in line ahead of common** — for dividends and in liquidation, but always behind creditors.
3. **Fixed payment = interest-rate risk** — rates up, price down; the only escape is a dividend that floats (adjustable-rate).
4. **Who does the feature help?** — investor-friendly features (cumulative, participating, convertible) mean a **lower** dividend; issuer-friendly features (callable) mean a **higher** one.

## Must-know facts

### Basics
- **Par value** — usually **$100**; dividend is a % of par (6% of $100 = $6/year, $1.50 quarterly).
- **Not guaranteed** — the board must declare preferred dividends, but unpaid preferred blocks any common dividend.
- **Usually lacks** — voting rights, preemptive rights, a maturity date, and growth potential (except convertible).
- **Dividends-received deduction** — corporations exclude much of the dividends they receive from other domestic corporations; makes preferred ideal for **corporate** investors.
- **Risks** — interest-rate risk and inflation (purchasing-power) risk from the fixed payment.

### Features

| Feature | Helps | Dividend vs. straight | Key point |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Straight** (non-cumulative) | — | Highest of basics | Skipped dividends are gone forever |
| **Cumulative** | Investor | Lower | All arrears + current paid before common |
| **Participating** | Investor | Lower | Fixed dividend + possible extra |
| **Convertible** | Investor | Lower | Converts to common; price tracks the common |
| **Callable** | Issuer | **Higher** | Called when rates **fall**; call/reinvestment risk |
| **Adjustable-rate** | Investor | Floats | Price stays near par; **least** rate risk |

- **Convertible** — conversion is not taxable; the only preferred with real growth potential.
- **Call protection** — a period when the issue can't be called; call premium partly compensates holders.

### Preferred vs. common vs. bonds

| | Common | Preferred | Bond |
|---|---|---|---|
| Type | Equity | Equity | Debt |
| Payment | Variable, not required | Fixed, not required | Fixed, **legally required** |
| Vote | Yes | Usually no | No |
| Maturity | None | None | Yes |
| Liquidation | Last | After creditors, before common | Before all stockholders |
| Rate sensitivity | Low/indirect | High | High |

### Suitability
- **Income, ahead of common** → preferred. **Income + growth** → convertible preferred. **Income + least rate risk** → adjustable-rate.
- **Not suitable** for capital appreciation (unless convertible) or for someone who needs a payment the issuer is legally bound to make (use a bond).

## Numbers to memorize

| What | Number |
|---|---|
| Typical preferred par | $100 (sometimes $25 or $50) |
| Dividend frequency | Quarterly |
| 6% on $100 par | $6/year = $1.50/quarter |

## Formulas

```
Annual dividend = Rate × Par
```
7% × $100 par = $7/year ($1.75 quarterly).

```
Current yield = Annual dividend ÷ Market price
```
$7 ÷ $87.50 = 8%.

```
Price after a rate change ≈ Annual dividend ÷ New market yield
```
$6 dividend, yields rise to 8% → $6 ÷ 0.08 = $75.

```
Cumulative payout before common = Arrears + Current
```
$5 cumulative, 2 years skipped → $5 + $5 + $5 = $15 before any common dividend.

```
Conversion ratio = Par ÷ Conversion price;  Parity of common = Preferred price ÷ Ratio
```
$100 par at $25 → 4 shares; preferred at $120 → common parity $30.

## Exam traps

- **Trap:** Preferred dividends are guaranteed like bond interest → **Truth:** they must be declared; only bond interest is a legal obligation.
- **Trap:** Callable preferred pays less because it's "safer" → **Truth:** callable helps the issuer, so it pays **more**.
- **Trap:** Issuers call preferred when rates rise → **Truth:** they call when rates **fall**, to refinance cheaper.
- **Trap:** Non-cumulative holders get missed dividends later → **Truth:** only cumulative preferred builds arrears.
- **Trap:** Preferred is the best choice for capital growth → **Truth:** fixed dividend caps growth; only convertible tracks the common.
- **Trap:** Preferred has the same rate risk whatever the type → **Truth:** adjustable-rate preferred stays near par.
- **Trap:** Individuals benefit most from preferred's tax treatment → **Truth:** the dividends-received deduction is for corporations.

## Memory hooks

- **"Cumulative catches up"** — arrears pile up and must be cleared first.
- **Investor perk = issuer pays less; issuer perk = issuer pays more.**
- **Adjustable = Anchored at par.**
- **Preferred sits in the middle** — bonds above it, common below.
