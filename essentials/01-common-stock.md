# Chapter 1: Common Stock

> **In one line:** Common stock is ownership with limited liability — the most growth potential, a vote, and a residual claim that is paid dead last.

## The big ideas

1. **Owners, not lenders** — stockholders get upside, votes and dividends only if the board declares them, so they are last in line in liquidation and carry the most risk.
2. **Splits and stock dividends change the slices, not the pie** — total value and ownership % never change, which lets you check any split math instantly.
3. **T+1 drives the calendar** — settlement, the ex-date (= record date) and the Reg T deadline all hang off trade date.
4. **Short = borrow and sell** — gain is capped at the sale price, loss is unlimited, and it must be done in margin.
5. **Fundamentals vs. technicals** — fundamental analysts read financial statements to value the company; technical analysts read charts.

## Must-know facts

### Shares and basics
- **Outstanding shares** — issued minus treasury; **treasury stock** has no vote and gets no dividend.
- **Limited liability** — maximum loss is the amount invested.
- **Market vs. business risk** — market (systematic) risk cannot be diversified away; business (non-systematic) risk can.
- **Penny stock** — unlisted stock under $5; special disclosure rules (SEC Rule 15g-9).
- **Cyclical vs. defensive** — cyclicals (autos, airlines) follow the economy; defensives (food, utilities, drugs) hold up in recessions.

### Stockholder rights
- **Vote** — elect the board; approve mergers, splits, charter changes. **Not** dividends (the board decides).
- **Statutory voting** — shares per seat. **Cumulative voting** — shares × seats, can stack on one candidate; favors small holders.
- **Proxy** — written authorization for someone else to vote your shares; SEC regulates under the '34 Act.
- **Preemptive right** — keep your % via a **rights offering**.
- **Inspect** — annual report, financials, shareholder list (not daily internal records).
- **Liquidation order** — secured creditors → admin costs/wages/taxes → unsecured creditors → subordinated debt → preferred → **common**.
- **Transfer agent** — keeps shareholder records, issues/cancels certificates. **Registrar** — ensures outstanding never exceeds authorized.
- **Street name** — registered to the broker-dealer, customer is the beneficial owner.

| | Rights | Warrants |
|---|---|---|
| Term | Weeks (30–60 days) | Years (can be perpetual) |
| Exercise price | **Below** market | **Above** market at issue |
| Given to | Existing shareholders | Bond/preferred buyers (sweetener) |

### Trading
- **Negotiable** (sell to another investor: stock, closed-end funds, ETFs) vs. **redeemable** (sell back to issuer: open-end funds, UITs).
- **Primary market** — proceeds go to the issuer ('33 Act). **Secondary market** — proceeds go to the selling investor ('34 Act).
- **Dividend dates (DERP)** — Declaration → Ex → Record → Payable. Buy **before** the ex-date to get the dividend; on the ex-date the price drops by the dividend.
- **Short sale** — margin account required; Reg SHO **locate** before selling; short seller pays dividends to the lender.
- **ADR** — US bank receipt for foreign shares; priced and paid in **US dollars**; holder bears **currency risk**; usually no vote; foreign tax credit possible.
- **Tender offer** — usually at a premium; tender only shares you own (**net long**).
- **Buyback** — outstanding shares become treasury; EPS rises.

### Fundamental analysis
- **Balance sheet** — a snapshot: assets = liabilities + equity. **Income statement** — performance over a period.
- **High P/E** → growth stock; **low P/E** → value stock.
- **Efficient market hypothesis** — weak (past prices), semi-strong (all public info), strong (even inside info) priced in.

## Formulas

```
Outstanding = Issued − Treasury
```
6M issued − 1M treasury = 5M outstanding.

```
Split A-for-B: new shares = shares × A/B; new price = price × B/A
```
3-for-2 on 100 shares @ $60 → 150 shares @ $40.

```
Cumulative votes = shares × seats
```
200 shares, 4 seats → 800 votes, all on one candidate if desired.

```
EPS = (Net income − Preferred dividends) ÷ Common shares;  P/E = Price ÷ EPS
```
($5M − $1M) ÷ 2M = $2.00; $40 ÷ $2 = P/E 20.

```
Dividend yield = Annual dividend ÷ Price;  Payout ratio = DPS ÷ EPS
```
$0.50 quarterly on a $40 stock → $2 ÷ $40 = 5%.

```
Current ratio = CA ÷ CL;  Quick ratio = (CA − Inventory) ÷ CL;  Working capital = CA − CL
Book value/share = (Equity − Preferred) ÷ Common shares
```
CA $900k (inventory $300k), CL $400k → current 2.25, quick 1.5, working capital $500k.

## Exam traps

- **Trap:** Shareholders vote on dividends → **Truth:** the board declares dividends; shareholders vote on splits, mergers and the board.
- **Trap:** Statutory voting helps small investors → **Truth:** cumulative voting does.
- **Trap:** Rights are long-term, priced above market → **Truth:** that's warrants; rights are short-term and below market.
- **Trap:** A split changes what your position is worth → **Truth:** value and ownership % are unchanged.
- **Trap:** Buy on the ex-date and you get the dividend → **Truth:** the seller gets it; buy before the ex-date.
- **Trap:** A short seller's max loss is the sale proceeds → **Truth:** that's max *gain*; max loss is unlimited.
- **Trap:** ADRs remove currency risk because they trade in dollars → **Truth:** a weaker foreign currency still lowers ADR value.
- **Trap:** The registrar keeps the shareholder list → **Truth:** that's the transfer agent; the registrar audits share counts.
- **Trap:** Diversification eliminates market risk → **Truth:** it only reduces business (non-systematic) risk.

## Memory hooks

- **DERP** — Declaration, Ex, Record, Payable.
- **Rights = Right now, Reduced price; Warrants = Wait years.**
- **Cumulative = Concentrate** your votes.
- **Short = Sky's the limit** for losses.
