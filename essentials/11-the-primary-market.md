# Chapter 11: The Primary Market

> **In one line:** Under the Securities Act of 1933, a new issue must be registered (the SEC checks completeness, never approves) and sold with a prospectus only after it is effective — unless an exemption applies.

## The big ideas

1. **Who gets the money defines the market** — in a primary offering the issuer receives the proceeds; in a secondary offering selling shareholders do.
2. **Who bears the risk defines the commitment** — firm commitment: underwriter buys it all; best efforts: issuer keeps unsold shares.
3. **Three registration periods, three rule sets** — pre-filing: nothing; cooling-off: gauge interest only; effective: sell with a final prospectus.
4. **Exempt security vs. exempt transaction** — some securities never register; others skip registration only because of how they're sold. Anti-fraud rules always apply.
5. **Insiders and IPOs are restricted** — Rule 144 limits resale of restricted/control stock; Rule 5130 bars industry insiders from buying IPOs.

## Must-know facts

### Players and the spread
- **SEC** — reviews the registration for completeness; never approves or passes on merit.
- **Managing underwriter** — forms the syndicate, runs the books, places the stabilizing bid.
- **Selling group** — sells for the concession only; no underwriting risk.
- **Spread** = manager's fee (smallest) + underwriting fee + selling concession (largest). Selling group earns only the concession.

### Commitments
| | Firm commitment | Best efforts |
|---|---|---|
| Capacity | Principal | Agent |
| Unsold-share risk | Underwriter | Issuer |
| Variations | Standby (rights offerings) | All-or-none, mini-max (escrow) |

- **Stabilizing bid** — at or below the POP, never above; disclosed in the prospectus.
- **Green shoe** — over-allotment up to 15% more shares.

### Offerings
- **IPO / follow-on** — both primary: new shares, issuer paid. **Secondary offering** — existing holders sell; no new shares. **Split** — both.
- **Shelf (Rule 415)** — register once, sell over up to 3 years.

### Registration periods
| Period | Allowed | Not allowed |
|---|---|---|
| Pre-filing | Normal business advertising | Any offer or solicitation |
| Cooling-off (≥ 20 days) | Red herring, tombstone ads, indications of interest, road shows, due diligence meeting | Sales, orders, money, marked-up red herrings |
| Effective | Sales; final prospectus by confirmation | — |

- **Red herring** — lacks final price and effective date (may show a range).
- **Indications of interest** — non-binding on both sides.
- **Deficiency letter** — pauses the 20-day clock.
- **Blue-sky** — coordination (with SEC filing; IPOs), qualification (full state review), notice filing (federal covered securities).

### Exemptions
- **Exempt securities** — US government/agency, munis, banks (not holding companies), commercial paper ≤ 270 days, non-profits, insurance and fixed annuities. Variable annuities are NOT exempt.
- **Reg D (private placement)** — 506(b): unlimited accredited + 35 non-accredited; PPM, restricted stock, Form D within 15 days.
- **Accredited investor** — net worth > $1M excluding home, or income > $200k ($300k joint) in each of the last 2 years.
- **Reg A+** — Tier 1 $20M, Tier 2 $75M; offering circular.
- **Rule 147** — one state, residents only; no resale to non-residents for 6 months.
- **Rule 144A** — resale of restricted securities to QIBs (≥ $100M). **Reg S** — offshore. **Crowdfunding** — $5M/yr.

### Rule 144
| | Restricted (non-affiliate) | Control (affiliate) |
|---|---|---|
| Holding | 6 months (reporting issuer) | None for registered shares |
| Volume per 90 days | None | Greater of 1% outstanding or 4-week avg weekly volume |
| Form 144 | No | Yes, at sale; good 90 days; not needed ≤ 5,000 sh and ≤ $50,000 |

- **Affiliate** — officer, director, 10%+ holder (and immediate family).

### FINRA new-issue and other rules
- **Rule 5130** — no common-stock IPO shares to restricted persons: member firms, their employees and immediate family, broker-dealer owners, PMs buying for themselves. Eligibility representation within 12 months; ≤ 10% restricted ownership is de minimis.
- **Rule 5131** — no spinning.
- **Penny stock** — unlisted, < $5: new customers sign a suitability statement and agreement.

## Formulas

```
Spread = POP − Proceeds to issuer = Manager's fee + Underwriting fee + Selling concession
```
POP $20, issuer gets $18.70, manager $0.15, underwriting $0.35 → concession = $1.30 − $0.50 = $0.80.

```
Rule 144 limit (90 days) = Greater of (1% × shares outstanding) or (average of last 4 weeks' volume)
```
40M shares (1% = 400,000); weekly volume 350k, 450k, 500k, 300k (avg 400,000) → 400,000 shares.

## Exam traps

- **Trap:** An effective registration means the SEC approved the issue → **Truth:** the SEC never approves; saying so is a violation.
- **Trap:** Cooling-off lets you take orders or deposits → **Truth:** only non-binding indications of interest.
- **Trap:** A secondary offering is secondary-market trading, or pays the company → **Truth:** it's a registered sale by existing holders, who keep the proceeds.
- **Trap:** Stabilizing bids may go above the POP → **Truth:** at or below only.
- **Trap:** Variable annuities are exempt like fixed annuities → **Truth:** variable annuities are securities and must register.
- **Trap:** Rule 144 volume is the lesser of the two tests → **Truth:** it's the greater.
- **Trap:** An employee's spouse may buy an IPO if paid with her own money → **Truth:** immediate family of member employees are restricted persons.
- **Trap:** Exempt from registration means exempt from anti-fraud → **Truth:** anti-fraud applies to every offer and sale.

## Memory hooks

- **"Cool off, don't sell."** Cooling-off = talk, tombstones, red herrings — no orders.
- **"90 / 25 / 40."** Unlisted IPO, listed IPO, unlisted follow-on.
- **"Greater of the two, every 90."** Rule 144 volume test resets each 90 days.
- **Concession is the Commission** — whoever sells gets the biggest slice.
