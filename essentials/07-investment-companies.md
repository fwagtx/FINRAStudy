# Chapter 7: Investment Companies

> **In one line:** Funds pool investor money under the 1940 Act, and most questions turn on how a fund is bought, priced, charged for and taxed.

## The big ideas

1. **Where you trade it defines the fund.** Open-end trades with the fund at NAV; closed-end and ETFs trade on an exchange at market price.
2. **Sales charges are a percentage of POP.** Every load and breakpoint calculation depends on that denominator.
3. **Share class follows amount and horizon.** A for big or long, C for short, B never for large sums.
4. **Funds are tax conduits.** Under Subchapter M the shareholder pays tax, even on reinvested distributions.
5. **Structure creates the risk.** UIT = no manager, ETN = no portfolio, leveraged ETF = daily reset.

## Must-know facts

### 1940 Act structure
- **Three types** — face-amount certificate companies, UITs, management companies (open- or closed-end).
- **Diversified** — 75-5-10: on 75% of assets, ≤ 5% in one issuer and ≤ 10% of its voting stock.
- **Board** — ≥ 40% independent; hires adviser, custodian, transfer agent.
- **Transfer agent** — issues/redeems shares, keeps records, sends distributions; **custodian** holds assets.
- **Shareholder vote** — needed to change the investment objective, adviser contract or 12b-1 plan.
- **Money market** — $1.00 NAV, not guaranteed; **index fund** — lowest expenses; **muni fund** — never in an IRA.

### Open-end vs closed-end vs ETF

| | Mutual fund | Closed-end | ETF |
|---|---|---|---|
| Shares | Continuous | Fixed, one IPO | Created/redeemed by APs |
| Traded | With the fund | Exchange | Exchange |
| Price | Next NAV (+ load) | Premium/discount to NAV | Market ≈ NAV |
| Cost | Sales charge | Commission | Commission + spread |
| Margin/short | No | Yes | Yes |

### Mutual fund transactions
- **Forward pricing** — fills at the next NAV computed after receipt (4:00 pm ET).
- **Redemption** — next NAV less any CDSC; paid within 7 days; taxable.
- **Prospectus** — always required (continuous offering).
- **Breakpoints** — individuals, spouses, minor children, trusts; NOT investment clubs.
- **Letter of intent** — reduced charge now; 13 months; backdate 90 days; new money only.
- **Rights of accumulation** — current value (appreciation counts) earns breakpoints on future purchases.
- **Breakpoint sale** — selling just below a breakpoint or splitting across families: prohibited.
- **Exchange privilege** — same family at NAV, no new load, still taxable.

### Share classes

| Class | Load | 12b-1 | Fits |
|---|---|---|---|
| **A** | Front-end, breakpoints | Low | Large or long-term |
| **B** | Declining CDSC → converts to A | ~1% | Small, long-term; never large |
| **C** | Level; 1% CDSC year 1 | ~1%, never converts | Short-term |

### Taxes
- **Subchapter M** — distribute ≥ 90% of net investment income → taxed only on what's kept.
- **Reinvested distributions** — taxable; raise cost basis.
- **LT gain distributions** — long-term regardless of shareholder's holding period.

### ETFs, ETNs, UITs, risk measures
- **Leveraged/inverse ETFs** — reset daily; not buy-and-hold.
- **ETN** — unsecured bank debt → issuer credit risk.
- **UIT** — fixed, unmanaged; no board/adviser; redeemable units; terminates.
- **Beta** — market risk (market = 1.0); **alpha** — return beyond what beta predicts; **standard deviation** — total volatility.

## Formulas

```
NAV = (Total assets − Total liabilities) ÷ Shares outstanding
```
($520M − $20M) ÷ 25M = $20.00.

```
POP = NAV ÷ (1 − Sales charge %)
```
19 ÷ 0.95 = $20.00.

```
Sales charge % = (POP − NAV) ÷ POP
```
(25 − 23) ÷ 25 = 8%.

```
Total return = (End NAV − Begin NAV + Distributions) ÷ Begin NAV
```
(42 − 40 + 1.20) ÷ 40 = 8%.

```
Expense ratio = Operating expenses ÷ Average net assets
```
$6M ÷ $800M = 0.75%.

## Exam traps

- **Trap:** Sales charge % uses NAV → **Truth:** it uses POP.
- **Trap:** Reinvested distributions aren't taxed → **Truth:** taxable; they raise basis.
- **Trap:** A fund held 3 months pays short-term gains → **Truth:** LT distributions are long-term regardless.
- **Trap:** ROA lowers charges on past purchases → **Truth:** ROA is prospective only.
- **Trap:** Appreciation counts toward an LOI → **Truth:** only new money counts.
- **Trap:** Class C suits a 20-year hold → **Truth:** its 12b-1 never ends — costliest long-term.
- **Trap:** Closed-end funds redeem at NAV → **Truth:** they trade at a premium or discount.
- **Trap:** A 3× ETF returns 3× the index for the year → **Truth:** only 3× the *daily* return.

## Memory hooks

- **POP is on top** — sales-charge math always divides by POP.
- **A = Amount, C = Close-out soon.**
- **LOI = new money, looking forward; ROA = rides appreciation.**
- **ETN = Exchange-Traded IOU** — a promise, so credit risk.
