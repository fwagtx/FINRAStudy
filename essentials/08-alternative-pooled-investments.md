# Chapter 8: Alternative Pooled Investments

> **In one line:** REITs, hedge funds and DPPs pool money outside the 1940 Act, and the exam tests who can buy them, what flows through for taxes, and how illiquid they are.

## The big ideas

1. **Losses flow through only in partnerships.** REITs pass income but never losses; DPPs (and hedge fund LPs) pass both — the most-tested distinction.
2. **Illiquidity is the headline risk.** Non-traded REITs, hedge fund lock-ups and DPPs can't be sold easily — suitability hinges on it.
3. **Private = accredited only.** Hedge funds are Reg D private placements, so no prospectus and only wealthy investors.
4. **Limited liability depends on staying passive.** An LP who manages risks becoming liable like the GP.

## Must-know facts

### REITs
- **Equity REIT** — owns properties; income from rents.
- **Mortgage REIT** — makes or buys mortgages; income from interest; most rate-sensitive.
- **Hybrid** — both.
- **Tax rule** — distribute ≥ 90% of taxable income and hold ≥ 75% of assets in real estate → no corporate tax.
- **Dividends** — taxed as ordinary income, not qualified rates.
- **Losses** — NOT passed through.
- **Listed REIT** — trades on exchanges, liquid; **non-traded REIT** — illiquid, high fees (often 10%+); **private REIT** — accredited only.
- **Not a 1940 Act investment company** — registered under the 1933 Act; trades like stock, not redeemable.
- **Rising rates** — hurt REIT prices, especially mortgage REITs.

### Hedge funds
- **Structure** — private limited partnership sold under Reg D; not 1933 Act registered; exempt from the 1940 Act.
- **Investors** — accredited only; offering memorandum instead of a prospectus.
- **3(c)(1)** — ≤ 100 investors; **3(c)(7)** — qualified purchasers only, no 100 cap.
- **Fees** — "2 and 20"; **high-water mark** = incentive fee only on new gains.
- **Lock-ups** — often 1 year+; redemptions only at set windows.
- **Fund of hedge funds** — can be sold to retail with lower minimums, but adds a second layer of fees.

### DPPs (limited partnerships)

| | General partner | Limited partner |
|---|---|---|
| Role | Manages, fiduciary duty | Passive capital |
| Liability | Unlimited | Limited to investment |
| Can | Run the business | Vote on major matters, inspect books, sue the GP |
| Cannot | Compete with the partnership | Participate in management |

- **Subscription agreement** — investor becomes an LP only when the GP accepts it.
- **Passive losses** — offset only passive income, not wages or portfolio income; unused losses carry forward.
- **Basis** — cash invested + share of recourse debt; losses can't exceed basis.
- **Depreciation** (real estate, equipment), **depletion** and **IDCs** (oil and gas) — key deductions.
- **Recapture** — depreciation taken may be taxed as ordinary income on sale.
- **Raw land** — appreciation only, no depreciation.
- **Oil and gas risk** — exploratory (wildcat) highest → developmental → income lowest.
- **Evaluation** — economic viability comes first, before tax benefits.
- **Not for IRAs** — tax benefits wasted.
- **Dissolution order** — secured creditors → general creditors → LPs → GP.

### Comparison

| | REIT | Hedge fund | DPP |
|---|---|---|---|
| Loss pass-through | No | Yes | Yes |
| Liquidity | Listed high; non-traded low | Low (lock-ups) | Very low |
| Who can buy | Anyone (listed) | Accredited | Income/net-worth minimums |

## Numbers to memorize

| What | Number |
|---|---|
| REIT distribution requirement | ≥ 90% of taxable income |
| REIT real estate assets / income | ≥ 75% |
| Hedge fund fees | 2% management + 20% of profits |
| Accredited investor net worth | > $1 million (excluding home) |
| Accredited investor income | > $200,000 single / $300,000 joint, last 2 years |
| 3(c)(1) investor limit | 100 |
| Qualified purchaser | $5 million+ in investments |
| GP minimum interest | 1% |
| Typical hedge fund lock-up | 1 year+ |
| Typical DPP holding period | 7–15 years |
| Non-traded REIT upfront fees | often 10%+ |

## Formulas

```
LP cost basis = Cash invested + Share of recourse debt
```
$50,000 cash + $30,000 recourse debt = $80,000 basis.

```
Hedge fund fee = 2% × assets + 20% × profit
```
$1M up 15%: $20,000 + $30,000 = $50,000 (one simple convention).

## Exam traps

- **Trap:** REIT losses flow through like a partnership → **Truth:** REITs pass through income only, never losses.
- **Trap:** REIT dividends get the 15% qualified rate → **Truth:** generally ordinary income.
- **Trap:** A REIT is an investment company like a closed-end fund → **Truth:** not under the 1940 Act.
- **Trap:** DPP losses can offset salary → **Truth:** passive losses offset only passive income.
- **Trap:** Signing the subscription agreement makes you an LP → **Truth:** the GP must accept it.
- **Trap:** Tax benefits are the top DPP consideration → **Truth:** economic viability comes first.
- **Trap:** Biggest DPP risk is tax law → **Truth:** illiquidity is the number-one risk.
- **Trap:** Hedge funds come with a prospectus → **Truth:** Reg D private placement — offering memorandum.

## Memory hooks

- **"REITs Report profits, Partnerships Pass losses."**
- **90/75** — REITs give away 90, keep 75 in real estate.
- **Wildcat = wild risk; income program = income now, lowest risk.**
- **GP = Gets Penalized (unlimited liability); LP = Lies low (passive).**
