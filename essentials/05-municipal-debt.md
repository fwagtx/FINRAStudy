# Chapter 5: Municipal Debt

> **In one line:** Munis trade lower yields for federally tax-free interest, so suitability hinges on tax bracket and account type, credit hinges on G.O. (taxes) vs. revenue (project), and the rules come from the MSRB but are enforced by others.

## The big ideas

1. **The tax exemption is the product.** Only the *interest* is federally tax-free; everything about who should buy munis (high bracket, taxable account) follows from that.
2. **Ask "what pays the bondholder?"** Taxes → G.O. (voter approval, debt limits, safer). Project user fees → revenue bond (feasibility study, coverage, higher yield).
3. **Compare with TEY, not raw yield.** A lower muni yield can beat a higher taxable yield once the bracket is applied.
4. **Exempt from registration, not from fraud rules.** Official statement replaces the prospectus; the MSRB writes rules but cannot enforce them.
5. **Notes are named for their repayment source.** TAN = taxes, RAN = revenues, BAN = future bond sale, GAN = grants.

## Must-know facts

### Taxation
- **Interest** — exempt from federal tax; state/local exempt usually only for **in-state** residents.
- **Territory bonds** (Puerto Rico, Guam, USVI) — triple tax-exempt for **all** US investors.
- **Capital gains** — fully taxable.
- **Private activity bonds** (stadiums, IDRs) — may be subject to **AMT**.
- **OID muni** — discount accretes tax-free; **market discount** — taxed as ordinary income when realized.
- **Premium muni** — premium must be amortized, lowers basis, not deductible; held to maturity at par → no gain, no loss.

### G.O. vs. revenue
| | **G.O. bond** | **Revenue bond** |
|---|---|---|
| Backing | Full faith, credit & taxing power | Revenue of a specific project |
| Voter approval / debt limits | Usually yes | No |
| Key analysis | Tax base, debt per capita, overlapping debt | Feasibility study, coverage ratio, covenants |
| Usual sale method | Competitive bid | Negotiated |
| Yield | Lower | Higher |

- **Ad valorem (property) tax** — main source for local G.O.s; states rely on income and sales taxes.
- **Overlapping debt** — debt of other jurisdictions (county, school district) on the same taxpayers.
- **Double-barreled** — revenue bond also backed by taxing power; analyzed as a G.O.
- **Rate covenant** — fees will cover expenses and debt service; **additional-bonds test** limits new parity debt.
- **Flow of funds (net revenue pledge)** — operations & maintenance first, then debt service, then reserves.

### Revenue-bond variants
- **IDR/IDB** — lease payments from a private company; credit = the company; usually AMT.
- **Special tax** — a specific tax (gas, tobacco, hotel); **special assessment** — charges on benefited properties only.
- **Moral obligation** — legislature *may* appropriate if short; not legally binding.
- **PHA/NHA** — backed by the US government; safest muni.

### Short-term notes
- **TAN** — future taxes; **RAN** — non-tax revenues; **TRAN** — both; **BAN** — proceeds of a future bond issue; **GAN** — grants; **CLN** — permanent financing after construction.
- **Ratings** — Moody's MIG 1–3 (VMIG for variable-rate demand notes); S&P SP-1 to SP-3.
- **VRDO** — rate resets daily/weekly with a **put** at par → treated as money-market.

### Regulation & trading
- **Official statement** — the disclosure document; final OS due by settlement (G-32).
- **Legal opinion** — bond counsel; **unqualified** = no reservations (good).
- **MSRB** — rulemaker, no enforcement; **FINRA, SEC, bank regulators** enforce.
- **EMMA** — official statements, disclosures, trade prices; **RTRS** receives trade reports.
- **Syndicate order priority** — pre-sale → group net → designated → member.
- **Quotes** — serial bonds on yield (basis); term bonds on price.
- **Key MSRB rules** — G-17 fair dealing; G-19 suitability; G-20 gifts; G-30 fair pricing/markups; G-32 OS delivery; G-37 political contributions.

### Suitability
- **Best buyer** — high bracket, taxable account, wants tax-free income, often in-state.
- **Poor fit** — low bracket, IRA/401(k), growth seekers.
- **Muni-specific risks** — legislative (tax-law change), liquidity (thin market), call (often 10 years after issue).

## Formulas

```
Tax-equivalent yield = Muni yield ÷ (1 − Tax bracket)
```
3.4% muni, 32% bracket → 3.4 ÷ 0.68 = 5.0%.

```
Tax-free equivalent = Taxable yield × (1 − Tax bracket)
```
6% corporate, 24% bracket → 6 × 0.76 = 4.56%.

```
Debt service coverage = Net revenue ÷ Annual debt service
```
($12M revenue − $4M O&M) ÷ $4M debt service = 2.0×.

```
Property tax = Assessed value ÷ 1,000 × Mills
```
$300,000 at 10 mills → $3,000.

## Exam traps

- **Trap:** Selling a muni at a profit is tax-free → **Truth:** only interest is exempt; capital gains are taxed.
- **Trap:** Any muni is state-tax-free for everyone → **Truth:** only in-state residents, except territory bonds.
- **Trap:** The MSRB enforces its rules → **Truth:** FINRA, the SEC and bank regulators enforce them.
- **Trap:** Revenue bonds need voter approval → **Truth:** G.O.s do; revenue bonds don't pledge taxes.
- **Trap:** Munis are a safe choice for an IRA → **Truth:** the exemption is wasted in a tax-deferred account.
- **Trap:** An MFP may give $250 to any candidate → **Truth:** only to one the MFP can vote for; any other gift triggers the ban.
- **Trap:** Market discount on a muni is tax-free like OID → **Truth:** market discount is ordinary income.
- **Trap:** A moral obligation bond legally binds the state → **Truth:** the legislature may, not must, appropriate.

## Memory hooks

- **G.O. = Go to the voters** (taxes pledged, so voters approve).
- **BAN = Bonds Are Next** — repaid by the coming bond sale.
- **TEY: divide to go up** — the taxable equivalent is always higher than the muni yield.
- **"Can't vote, can't give"** — G-37.
- **Unqualified opinion = unquestioned** (good).
