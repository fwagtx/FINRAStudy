# Chapter 4: Corporate Debt

> **In one line:** Corporate debt is ranked by what backs it and when it matures — short-term discount paper, secured vs. unsecured bonds, convertibles that track the stock, and a strict pecking order in bankruptcy.

## The big ideas

1. **Collateral sets safety and yield.** Secured bonds (real estate, equipment, securities) are safer and pay less than unsecured debentures; subordinated debt pays the most because it stands last among creditors.
2. **Money market = one year or less, bought at a discount.** If you know the issuer (corporation, bank, dealer, Treasury) you can pick the instrument.
3. **A convertible is a bond with a stock option inside.** Lower coupon in exchange for upside; its price follows the bond floor when the stock is low and parity when the stock is high.
4. **Liquidation is strict priority.** Each class is paid in full before the next gets anything; creditors always beat owners.
5. **"Euro" means "outside the home country," not the euro.** Where a bond is issued decides SEC registration; the currency decides currency risk.

## Must-know facts

### Money-market instruments
| Instrument | Issuer | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **Commercial paper** | Corporations | Unsecured; ≤ 270 days → exempt from 1933 Act registration |
| **Negotiable (jumbo) CD** | Banks | $100,000+; tradable; interest at maturity |
| **Banker's acceptance** | Bank-guaranteed draft | Finances **import/export** |
| **Repo** | Dealers | Sell now, buy back higher = collateralized loan; reverse repo = other side |
| **Fed funds** | Banks | Overnight loans of excess reserves |

### Secured vs. unsecured bonds
- **Mortgage bond** — backed by real estate/plant; common for utilities.
- **Equipment trust certificate** — backed by *movable* equipment (railcars, aircraft); trustee holds title.
- **Collateral trust bond** — backed by securities the issuer owns, held by a trustee.
- **Debenture** — unsecured, full faith and credit of issuer; holders are general creditors.
- **Subordinated debenture** — ranks behind other debentures; higher yield; often convertible.
- **Guaranteed bond** — another company (usually the parent) guarantees; quality = guarantor's.
- **Income (adjustment) bond** — pays only if earned; issued in reorganization; **trades flat**.
- **Debt vs. equity for the issuer** — interest is tax-deductible and doesn't dilute; but it's a fixed obligation that adds leverage.

### Convertibles
- **Lower coupon** than straight debt; issuer saves interest and conversion retires the debt.
- **Investment value (bond floor)** — when stock is far below conversion price, the bond trades on its yield.
- **Anti-dilution** — conversion price adjusts for splits/stock dividends.
- **Conversion is not taxable** — bond's cost basis carries to the shares.
- **Forced conversion** — issuer calls the bond when conversion value exceeds the call price.
- **Arbitrage** — bond price below parity → buy bond, convert.

### Liquidation order
1. **Secured creditors** (to the value of collateral; any shortfall becomes a general claim)
2. **Administrative costs, wages, taxes**
3. **General creditors** (debentures, suppliers)
4. **Subordinated debentures**
5. **Preferred stock**
6. **Common stock**
- **Chapter 11** — reorganize and keep operating; **Chapter 7** — liquidate.

### Trading & bank products
- **Corporates trade mostly OTC**; reported to **TRACE** within 15 minutes; markups disclosed on retail confirms.
- **"XYZ 5s of '38"** — 5% coupon, matures 2038, $25 every six months.
- **FDIC** — $250,000 per depositor, per bank, per ownership category; CDs are not SIPC-covered.
- **Brokered CDs** — FDIC-insured, may be callable, secondary-market price can be below par.
- **Market-linked CDs** — principal protected only if held to maturity.

### Offshore bonds
| | Issued | Currency | SEC-registered? |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Eurodollar bond** | Outside US | USD | No; pays **annual** interest |
| **Yankee bond** | In US, foreign issuer | USD | Yes |
- **Eurodollars** — USD deposits in banks outside the US; no FDIC, no US reserve requirements; benchmark now SOFR (replaced LIBOR).

## Formulas

```
Conversion ratio = Par ÷ Conversion price
```
$1,000 ÷ $40 = 25 shares.

```
Parity price of bond = Stock price × Conversion ratio
Parity price of stock = Bond price ÷ Conversion ratio
```
Ratio 25, stock $44 → bond parity $1,100; bond $1,050 → stock parity $42.

```
Accrued interest = Annual interest × Days ÷ 360   (count to the day BEFORE settlement)
```
6% bond, J&J 1, settles Mar 16: 30 + 30 + 15 = 75 days → $60 × 75/360 = $12.50.

```
Dollar price = Quote (% of par) × $10
```
97 ⅝ → $976.25.

## Exam traps

- **Trap:** Commercial paper stops at 270 days because of a Fed rule → **Truth:** 270 days or less is exempt from SEC registration.
- **Trap:** Equipment trust certificates are backed by real estate → **Truth:** movable equipment; mortgage bonds use real estate, collateral trust bonds use securities.
- **Trap:** Subordinated debentures are paid before ordinary debentures → **Truth:** they rank behind general creditors, just ahead of preferred.
- **Trap:** Converting a bond to stock is a taxable sale → **Truth:** no tax; the basis carries over.
- **Trap:** Income bonds pay accrued interest like other bonds → **Truth:** they trade flat.
- **Trap:** Corporate bonds are quoted in 32nds → **Truth:** corporates in 1/8ths; Treasuries in 32nds.
- **Trap:** Eurodollar bonds are SEC-registered or carry currency risk for a dollar investor → **Truth:** unregistered, and no currency risk in USD.
- **Trap:** A $1 million jumbo CD is fully FDIC-insured → **Truth:** insurance still caps at $250,000.

## Memory hooks

- **"BA = Buy Abroad"** — banker's acceptances finance foreign trade.
- **"ETC = Equipment That Can move."**
- **Lenders before owners; collateral before everyone** — common stock is always last.
- **Yankee comes to America** (registered here); **Eurodollar leaves America** (unregistered).
- **Parity: stock × ratio up, bond ÷ ratio down.**
