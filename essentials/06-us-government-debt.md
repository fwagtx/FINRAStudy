# Chapter 6: US Government Debt (and the Economy)

> **In one line:** Treasuries carry no credit risk and are federally taxed but state-exempt; agency MBS add prepayment risk; and the Fed (monetary) and Congress/President (fiscal) steer rates and the economy that every other price reacts to.

## The big ideas

1. **Treasuries are the risk-free benchmark — for credit only.** They still carry interest-rate, inflation and reinvestment risk; every other yield is a spread over them.
2. **Treasury taxation is the mirror of munis.** Federal tax yes, state and local no — except mortgage-backed agency securities, taxed at every level.
3. **Mortgage securities move against the borrower's incentive.** Rates fall → homeowners refinance (prepayment risk); rates rise → payments slow (extension risk).
4. **Fed buys = money in = easier.** Every Fed tool either adds reserves (ease) or drains them (tighten); open-market operations are the everyday lever.
5. **Who acts decides the policy type.** Fed → monetary (fast, money supply/rates); Congress & President → fiscal (slow, taxes/spending).

## Must-know facts

### Treasury products
| Security | Maturity | Key fact |
|---|---|---|
| **T-bill** | ≤ 52 weeks | Discount, matures at par; quoted on discount yield |
| **T-note** | 2–10 years | Semiannual coupon; quoted in 32nds |
| **T-bond** | 20–30 years | Semiannual coupon; quoted in 32nds |
| **TIPS** | 5, 10, 30 years | Principal adjusts with CPI; fixed rate × adjusted principal; phantom income taxed yearly; get greater of adjusted or original principal at maturity |
| **STRIPS** | Various | Zero-coupon, **direct** Treasury obligation; no reinvestment risk; most rate-sensitive |
| **Treasury receipts** | Various | Dealer-created zeros; **not** direct Treasury obligations |
| **FRN** | 2 years | Resets weekly to 13-week bill rate |
| **Savings bonds (EE, I)** | Up to 30 years | **Non-negotiable**; tax-deferred until redeemed |

- **Auctions** — non-competitive bids accept the auction yield and are filled first; competitive bids name a yield.
- **Book-entry only**; minimum **$100**; settle T+1; accrued interest **actual/365**.
- **T-bill quotes** — higher discount yield = lower price; the bid yield is higher than the ask yield.
- **10-year Treasury** — benchmark for mortgage rates; corporate spread over Treasuries = credit risk.

### Agencies & MBS
- **Ginnie Mae (GNMA)** — part of HUD; **only** agency with full faith and credit backing; monthly payments; $25,000 minimum.
- **Fannie Mae / Freddie Mac** — publicly traded GSEs in conservatorship; implied, not legal, backing.
- **FHLB & Farm Credit** — interest exempt from state/local tax.
- **MBS & CMO interest** — taxable at federal, state and local levels; monthly payments of interest (taxable) plus principal (return of capital).
- **CMO tranches** — **PAC** most predictable; **companion/support** absorbs prepayment swings; **Z-tranche** accrues like a zero.

### Monetary policy & rates
- **Fed's dual mandate** — stable prices and maximum employment.
- **FOMC** — 12 voters: 7 governors + NY Fed president + 4 rotating regional presidents; meets 8 times a year.
- **Money supply** — M1 = currency + checking (+ savings); M2 = M1 + small time deposits + retail money funds.

| Rate | Who lends to whom | Set by |
|---|---|---|
| **Fed funds** | Bank → bank, overnight | Market; FOMC sets **target range** |
| **Discount rate** | Fed → member banks | Fed **directly** |
| **Broker call** | Bank → broker-dealer (margin loans) | Banks |
| **Prime** | Bank → best corporate customers | Banks |

| Fed tool | Ease | Tighten | Note |
|---|---|---|---|
| **Open-market operations** | Buy securities | Sell securities | Most used |
| **Discount rate** | Lower | Raise | Signal |
| **Reserve requirement** | Lower | Raise | Most powerful, least used |
| **Interest on reserves** | Lower | Raise | Steers fed funds |

- **QE** — buy long Treasuries/MBS to push long rates down; **QT** — let holdings run off.

### Economy & fiscal policy
- **Business cycle** — expansion → peak → contraction → trough.
- **GDP** — output inside the US regardless of owner (GNP = US-owned output anywhere).
- **CPI** — consumer inflation; **PPI** — wholesale, tends to lead CPI. **Stagflation** — stagnant growth + high inflation.
- **Leading** — stock market, building permits, durable-goods orders, initial jobless claims, M2, yield curve.
- **Coincident** — GDP, industrial production, personal income, payrolls.
- **Lagging** — unemployment rate, CPI, prime rate, corporate profits, consumer debt.
- **Weak dollar** helps exporters; **strong dollar** helps importers and US travelers.
- **Theories** — Keynesian = government spending; Monetarist = money supply; Supply-side = cut taxes and regulation.
- **Fiscal policy** — taxes and spending; deficits can crowd out private borrowing.

## Exam traps

- **Trap:** Treasury interest is state-taxable → **Truth:** federal only; state and local exempt.
- **Trap:** Ginnie Mae interest is state-exempt like Treasuries → **Truth:** MBS interest is taxed at all levels.
- **Trap:** Fannie Mae and Freddie Mac carry full US backing → **Truth:** only Ginnie Mae does.
- **Trap:** The Fed sells securities to stimulate → **Truth:** it buys to ease, sells to tighten.
- **Trap:** The discount rate is what banks charge each other → **Truth:** that's fed funds; discount is the Fed lending to banks.
- **Trap:** Reserve requirements are the most-used tool → **Truth:** open-market operations; reserve changes are least used.
- **Trap:** The unemployment rate is leading → **Truth:** it lags; initial jobless claims lead.
- **Trap:** MBS prepayment risk rises when rates rise → **Truth:** when rates fall; rising rates bring extension risk.
- **Trap:** A higher T-bill quote means a higher price → **Truth:** bills quote on yield; higher yield = lower price.

## Memory hooks

- **Munis and Treasuries swap exemptions** — munis skip federal tax, Treasuries skip state tax.
- **Ginnie = Government** — the only one with the government's guarantee.
- **"Buy = Bigger money supply."**
- **Fed funds = bank to bank; Discount = Fed's window.**
- **Monetary = Money (Fed); Fiscal = Federal budget (Congress).**
